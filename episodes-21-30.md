# New Three Kingdoms (新三国) - Episodes 21-30
*From the Blood Edict to the Battle of Guandu: Loyalty Tested, Empires Shaken*

These ten episodes form one of the series' most dramatically rich arcs. They trace the exposure and destruction of the conspiracy against Cao Cao, the separation and reunion of the three sworn brothers, Guan Yu's legendary service under Cao Cao, and the decisive Battle of Guandu that breaks Yuan Shao's power and establishes Cao Cao as the dominant force in northern China. By the arc's end, Liu Bei has lost everything and must start over in the south under Liu Biao's protection.

---

## Episode 21: The Blood Edict Unravels

Cao Cao's advisors Cheng Yu and Guo Jia confront him about the blunder of allowing Liu Bei to leave the capital with an army. Cao Cao sends troops to recall Liu Bei, but Liu Bei refuses to comply. Meanwhile, the warlord Yuan Shu dies of starvation in a roadside temple, and the Imperial Jade Seal falls briefly into Liu Bei's hands before he sends it to Cao Cao as a gesture of nominal loyalty. When Liu Bei's ally Che Zhou is killed by Guan Yu, Cao Cao flies into a rage and suffers one of his debilitating headaches.

Elsewhere, Liu Bei writes to Yuan Shao proposing a joint campaign against Cao Cao. Yuan Shao seizes the opportunity and begins marshaling 300,000 troops at Liyang. Back in the capital, the physician Ji Ping and the imperial kinsman Dong Cheng attempt to assassinate Cao Cao by poison, acting on a secret edict (the "blood edict," or 衣带诏) hidden in Emperor Xian's belt. Their plot is discovered, and at a banquet, Ji Ping is brought before the assembled officials to expose his fellow conspirators.

**Historical significance:** The "blood edict" incident (衣带诏) is attested in historical sources, though its exact details are debated. Dong Cheng was a real figure who conspired against Cao Cao around 199-200 AD and was executed for it. The series dramatizes the discovery as a banquet confrontation for theatrical effect. Ji Ping (吉平) is largely a fictional character from the novel. Historically, Emperor Xian was by this point almost entirely powerless, and Cao Cao's dominance over the court was the political reality that drove these desperate conspiracies. This episode sets the stage for the pivotal Cao Cao vs. Yuan Shao conflict that will define the next decade of Chinese history.

---

## Episode 22: Liu Bei's Defeat and Despair

Ji Ping, the physician-conspirator, refuses to betray his co-conspirators even under torture and ultimately kills himself by smashing his head against a pillar. However, a servant from the Dong Cheng household betrays his master. Dong Cheng curses Cao Cao defiantly, and Cao Cao orders the extermination of his entire clan. Cao Cao then enters the palace and has Consort Dong (Dong Cheng's pregnant kinswoman) killed, further tightening his grip on the emperor.

With the conspiracy crushed, Cao Cao commits his full army to an assault on Liu Bei's position at Xuzhou, intending to destroy Liu Bei before turning to face Yuan Shao. Liu Bei sends urgent letters to Yuan Shao begging him to attack the now-emptied capital of Xuchang, but Yuan Shao, distracted by his youngest son's illness, refuses to move. Liu Bei gambles on a desperate night raid against Cao Cao's camp, but Cao Cao has anticipated the attack. Liu Bei walks into an ambush and barely escapes with his life while Cao Cao seizes Xuzhou. Alone and shattered, Liu Bei reaches a deep ravine and contemplates suicide, but is pulled back from the brink.

**Historical significance:** The destruction of the Dong Cheng conspiracy and Cao Cao's subsequent attack on Liu Bei at Xuzhou are historical events from 200 AD. The novel and series amplify Liu Bei's despair for dramatic purposes. Historically, Liu Bei's defeat at Xuzhou scattered his forces: Guan Yu was captured, and Liu Bei fled north to Yuan Shao. The killing of Consort Dong (董贵人), Dong Cheng's pregnant kinswoman, is recorded in the histories and represents one of Cao Cao's most brazen acts of intimidation against the imperial household. Some episode guides mistakenly identify the victim as Empress Fu (伏皇后), but her death at Cao Cao's hands occurs much later in the series (around episode 61, corresponding to events of 214 AD). Note: the series telescopes events that historically unfolded over several months into a single dramatic sequence.

## Episode 23: Guan Yu Surrenders on Three Conditions

Yuan Shao's youngest son recovers from illness, lifting the warlord's spirits. He reconciles with his advisor Xu You and sends him to Xuzhou to gather intelligence, while simultaneously preparing his own massive army to march on the capital. Liu Bei, learning of these developments, sighs at the cruelty of fate and returns to serve under Yuan Shao, who receives him warmly.

In Xuzhou, Cao Cao devises a plan to win over Guan Yu rather than kill him. Through the persistent persuasion of Zhang Liao (Guan Yu's friend and Cao Cao's trusted general), Guan Yu agrees to a conditional surrender. He stipulates three famous conditions: he surrenders to the Han Emperor, not to Cao Cao personally; Liu Bei's wives must be protected and treated with full respect; and the moment Guan Yu learns of Liu Bei's whereabouts, he must be free to leave. Cao Cao accepts all three terms. He showers Guan Yu with gifts and honors, but Guan Yu remains emotionally distant, his mind fixed on finding his brother. To cement Guan Yu's loyalty, Cao Cao presents him with the legendary Red Hare horse.

**Historical significance:** Guan Yu's conditional surrender to Cao Cao is rooted in historical sources, though the specific "three conditions" (降汉不降曹) are a famous embellishment from the novel. Historically, Guan Yu did serve Cao Cao briefly after the fall of Xuzhou and performed notable military service. The gifting of Red Hare (赤兔马, originally Lu Bu's horse) is from the novel tradition. This episode establishes the central moral tension of the arc: Guan Yu must balance genuine gratitude toward Cao Cao against unshakable loyalty to Liu Bei, a dilemma that Chinese culture has celebrated for centuries as the essence of "yi" (义, righteousness).

---

## Episode 24: Guan Yu Slays Yan Liang and Wen Chou

Upon receiving Red Hare, Guan Yu reveals where his heart truly lies by declaring that the horse can travel a thousand li in a day, meaning he can find his brother sooner. Cao Cao's generals urge him to execute Guan Yu, but Cao Cao refuses, still hoping to win the warrior's permanent allegiance. Zhang Liao visits Guan Yu once more to convey Cao Cao's generosity, but Guan Yu simply reaffirms his commitment to Liu Bei.

At Yuan Shao's camp, Liu Bei pushes for an attack on Cao Cao, and Yuan Shao agrees. Yuan Shao sends his champion general Yan Liang to lead the vanguard. When Yan Liang cuts down two of Cao Cao's officers in succession, Guan Yu volunteers to face him. In a breathtaking single combat at the Battle of Baima (White Horse), Guan Yu charges through the enemy lines and kills Yan Liang. Yuan Shao is furious and blames Liu Bei, threatening to execute him. When Guan Yu subsequently kills the general Wen Chou as well, Liu Bei spots the banner reading "Han Shou Marquis" and realizes his brother is fighting for the other side. Yuan Shao orders Liu Bei's execution, but Liu Bei persuades Yuan Shao to let him write to Guan Yu and bring him over to their side.

**Historical significance:** Guan Yu's slaying of Yan Liang at the Battle of Baima (白马之战, 200 AD) is one of the few combat feats directly confirmed by the historical record (Sanguozhi). It was a decisive moment that broke Yuan Shao's vanguard. The killing of Wen Chou, however, is historically attributed to Cao Cao's broader tactical maneuver rather than to Guan Yu personally. The novel and series give Guan Yu credit for both kills to heighten his legend. Guan Yu was indeed awarded the title "Marquis of Han Shou" (汉寿亭侯) for his service, a title he cherished because it came from the Han Emperor rather than from Cao Cao.

---

## Episode 25: A Thousand Li Ride Alone

Guan Yu receives Liu Bei's letter and immediately seeks an audience with Cao Cao to take his leave, but Cao Cao hangs an "Unavailable" sign on his gate and refuses to see him. Zhang Liao likewise turns Guan Yu away. Undeterred, Guan Yu returns to his residence, informs Liu Bei's two wives of the situation, formally returns his seal of office and all the gold Cao Cao has given him, and rides out of the city.

In a famous scene, Cao Cao has a change of heart and personally leads his generals to see Guan Yu off at the city outskirts. He presents Guan Yu with gold and a brocade robe as farewell gifts. Guan Yu, deeply moved, dismounts and bows to Cao Cao in gratitude before departing. Watching him go, Cao Cao realizes the terrifying power of true loyalty.

Meanwhile, Zhang Fei has taken refuge in a small city, where he has overthrown the local magistrate and installed himself as ruler. He recruits and trains a scrappy fighting force while sending scouts to search for Liu Bei and Guan Yu. When he learns that Guan Yu has been serving under Cao Cao, Zhang Fei erupts in fury and weeps with rage. Along the road, Guan Yu begins his legendary journey of "crossing five passes and slaying six generals" (过五关斩六将) as he fights his way through Cao Cao's territory, escorting Liu Bei's wives to safety.

**Historical significance:** Guan Yu's departure from Cao Cao's service is historical; the Sanguozhi confirms he left to rejoin Liu Bei after learning of his brother's location. However, the dramatic "hanging up the seal and returning the gold" (挂印封金) is a novelistic embellishment, and the famous "crossing five passes, slaying six generals" sequence is entirely fictional. No such gauntlet of checkpoints existed in history. The novel invented this legendary journey to cement Guan Yu's image as the supreme embodiment of loyalty. In the series, these scenes are visually spectacular and emotionally resonant, representing the climax of Guan Yu's moral arc within Cao Cao's camp.

---

## Episode 26: Reunion at Ancient City

Guan Yu arrives at the ancient city (古城) where Zhang Fei has established himself. Zhang Fei charges out to confront his brother, furious at what he sees as a betrayal for serving Cao Cao. After a tense standoff and some dramatic complications, the two brothers reconcile and put their differences behind them.

Liu Bei, who has slipped away from Yuan Shao's camp, encounters Zhao Yun on the road. The three sworn brothers and Zhao Yun converge on the ancient city for an emotional reunion. Their small but fiercely loyal band is whole again.

The narrative then shifts north, where Yuan Shao's three sons arrive with reinforcements for the coming war against Cao Cao. A fierce dispute breaks out among the brothers over who should command the army, but Yuan Shao settles the matter by taking personal command. The episode ends with Yuan Shao leading a massive host of 700,000 soldiers south toward Guandu for the decisive confrontation with Cao Cao.

**Historical significance:** The "Reunion at Gucheng" (古城聚义) is a beloved scene from the novel but has no basis in the historical record. Liu Bei did eventually reunite with Guan Yu and Zhang Fei, but the dramatic circumstances are fictional. The quarrel among Yuan Shao's three sons (Yuan Tan, Yuan Xi, Yuan Shang), however, foreshadows a very real historical problem: after Yuan Shao's death, his sons' infighting would destroy the Yuan clan from within. Yuan Shao's army at Guandu was historically large (estimates range from 100,000 to several hundred thousand), though the 700,000 figure is a novelistic exaggeration.

---

## Episode 27: The Battle of Guandu Begins

On the eve of the great battle, Yuan Shao cuts his own hand and uses his blood to consecrate a sacrifice to heaven and earth, delivering a rousing speech that fires up his army. But a shadow falls over the proceedings: his imprisoned advisor Tian Feng smuggles out a blood-written memorial desperately urging Yuan Shao not to march. Yuan Shao, enraged, vows to punish Tian Feng after he has crushed Cao Cao.

At Xuchang, Cao Cao can muster only 70,000 elite troops against Yuan Shao's overwhelming numbers. He stakes everything on winning the first engagement. When the two armies face each other across the plain at Guandu, Cao Cao makes a stunning gambit: he proposes surrendering the emperor to Yuan Shao in exchange for peace. Yuan Shao is tempted and begins negotiating for cities and territory. But Cao Cao is stalling. While talk continues at the front, Cao Ren, Xu Chu, and other commanders are quietly encircling Yuan Shao's position from the flanks.

At noon, Cao Cao springs the trap. Under the blazing sun, Yuan Shao's troops are hammered by wave after wave of arrows and cannot take cover. War horns blare from every direction as Cao Cao raises his sword and shouts his declaration: he fights by imperial mandate to destroy the rebel Yuan Shao.

**Historical significance:** The Battle of Guandu (官渡之战, 200 AD) is one of the most consequential battles in Chinese history, a classic case of a smaller, disciplined force defeating a numerically superior enemy through superior strategy. The series dramatizes the opening clash with considerable theatrical license. The historical Cao Cao did not offer to surrender the emperor as a ruse, and the actual battle opened with months of positional warfare rather than a single dramatic engagement. The detail about Tian Feng's warning is historically grounded: Tian Feng did advise against the campaign and was imprisoned for it. His story is one of the great tragedies of the Three Kingdoms period.

---

## Episode 28: Xu You Defects to Cao Cao

Despite Yuan Shao's numerical advantage, four rounds of fierce combat end in his defeat. His forces retreat in disorder, and Liu Bei helps Yuan Shao escape from Cao Cao's pursuing cavalry. Back at camp, Yuan Shao receives slanderous reports about his imprisoned advisor Tian Feng and orders Tian Feng's execution. Yet Yuan Shao still commands vast reserves and the war is far from over.

On Cao Cao's side, the victory comes at a heavy cost. His supplies are nearly exhausted, and he writes anxiously to his advisor Xun Yu for guidance. Meanwhile, Xu You, one of Yuan Shao's chief strategists, discovers the dire state of Cao Cao's provisions and proposes a bold two-pronged attack: feint against Cao Cao's camp while sending a strike force to seize the poorly defended capital of Xuchang. Yuan Shao, suspicious and stubborn, refuses. When Tian Feng's dying blood letter warns Yuan Shao that Xu You cannot be trusted, it becomes a self-fulfilling prophecy. Yuan Shao expels Xu You from his camp. That night, Xu You slips away and defects to Cao Cao, carrying with him the intelligence that will change the course of the war.

**Historical significance:** Xu You's (许攸) defection to Cao Cao is one of the great turning points of the Guandu campaign and is well documented historically. Xu You revealed to Cao Cao the location of Yuan Shao's grain depot at Wuchao (乌巢), which became the target of the war's decisive strike. The execution of Tian Feng (田丰) is also historical. He had warned Yuan Shao against fighting at Guandu and was killed in prison. Sima Guang's Zizhi Tongjian records Tian Feng's prescient final words: his master would never forgive him for having been right. The series dramatizes Tian Feng's death with a blood letter, which is a novelistic addition.

---

## Episode 29: Fire at Wuchao

Cao Cao rushes out barefoot to welcome Xu You, overjoyed at his old friend's arrival. Xu You asks how long Cao Cao's grain can last. Cao Cao lies repeatedly, claiming one year, then half a year, three months, half a month, ten days. Xu You laughs and calls him the "ancestor of all villains." Cao Cao finally admits the truth: he has only three days of supplies left. Xu You lays out the plan: a night raid on Wuchao (乌巢), the lightly guarded depot where Yuan Shao stores all his grain and supplies. Burn Wuchao, and Yuan Shao's army will collapse.

Cao Cao acts immediately. He personally leads five thousand picked cavalry on a nighttime forced march to Wuchao. The garrison, under the negligent and drunken general Chunyu Qiong, is caught completely off guard. Cao Cao himself draws his sword and fights in the assault. The fires consume tens of thousands of bushels of grain and all of Yuan Shao's supplies. Cao Cao loads his horses with captured provisions and withdraws triumphantly.

The aftermath is swift. Liu Bei's position at his small stronghold near Gucheng falls to Cao Cao's advancing forces, and Liu Bei flees south to Jingzhou to seek refuge with Liu Biao. Despite opposition from the powerful Cai Mao, Liu Biao receives Liu Bei warmly, and the two bond over their shared imperial lineage. Liu Biao assigns Liu Bei to garrison the small frontier town of Xinye. It is a humble posting, and Liu Bei departs with a heavy heart, while Cai Mao orders his men to watch Guan Yu, Zhang Fei, and Zhao Yun closely.

**Historical significance:** The night raid on Wuchao (乌巢) in the autumn of 200 AD was the decisive turning point of Guandu and one of the most celebrated surprise attacks in Chinese military history. The historical details largely match the dramatization: Xu You revealed Wuchao's location, Cao Cao personally led the strike, and Chunyu Qiong's garrison was overwhelmed. The series compresses subsequent events: historically, Liu Bei did not go to Jingzhou immediately after Guandu. He remained in Yuan Shao's orbit for a time before making his way south. Liu Biao's reception of Liu Bei and the assignment to Xinye are historical, as is Cai Mao's hostility. The Wuchao raid stands as proof of a principle that resonates throughout the Three Kingdoms: in war, logistics and intelligence matter more than raw numbers.

---

## Episode 30: Cao Cao's Northern Dominion

Yuan Shao, reeling from the Wuchao disaster, launches a desperate counterattack on Cao Cao's main camp, reasoning that Cao Cao must have left it empty to raid Wuchao. He is wrong. The camp is an elaborate trap: flags wave over empty tents, and the moment Yuan Shao's forces pour inside, they are struck from all sides by crossbow bolts and catapult stones from hidden positions. In the chaos and darkness, Yuan Shao's army is shattered. Yuan Shao fights his way out with his sons' help, only to discover that Cao Cao has seized his own camp in his absence. Attacked again by volleys of arrows, Yuan Shao flees in complete rout. He vomits blood and his health begins to fail.

In the north, Cao Cao consolidates his conquest of Ji Province and the rest of Yuan Shao's former territories. His general Xu Chu kills the boastful Xu You, who has been loudly taking credit for Cao Cao's victory at Guandu. With six of the nine provinces now under his control, Cao Cao announces a year of military rest before his next campaign to unify the realm.

The episode then shifts south to Jingzhou, where Liu Qi (Liu Biao's elder son) confides in Liu Bei about the danger he faces from his stepmother Lady Cai and her brother Cai Mao, who are scheming to install the younger son as heir. Liu Bei advises Liu Biao to name Liu Qi as his successor, undermining the Cai family's influence. But Lady Cai overhears this counsel, and she and Cai Mao begin plotting to assassinate Liu Bei before he can become a threat.

**Historical significance:** Yuan Shao's final defeat and the collapse of his power are historical. After Guandu, Yuan Shao retreated to Hebei a broken man and died in 202 AD, likely from illness compounded by despair. His sons' subsequent civil war allowed Cao Cao to absorb the entire Yuan territory by 207 AD, though the series compresses this years-long process. The killing of Xu You by Xu Chu is from the novel; historically, Xu You was executed by Cao Cao's order, though the circumstances are disputed. The Jingzhou subplot introduces a historically significant power struggle: Liu Biao's indecision about his succession between Liu Qi and Liu Cong, manipulated by the Cai clan, would ultimately hand Jingzhou to Cao Cao after Liu Biao's death in 208 AD. This sets the stage for the next great chapter of the story, the flight south and the Battle of Red Cliffs.
