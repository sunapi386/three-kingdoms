# New Three Kingdoms (新三国) - Episodes 51-60
*The Jingzhou Gambit: Marriage, Betrayal, and the Death of Zhou Yu*

These ten episodes trace one of the most celebrated arcs in the Three Kingdoms saga: the struggle over Jingzhou between Sun Quan and Liu Bei, the famous "beauty trap" marriage scheme, Zhou Yu's final humiliation and death, the rise of Pang Tong, and the opening moves of Cao Cao's succession crisis and the Ma Teng conspiracy. The strategic center of gravity is Jingzhou, the crossroads province that all three kingdoms need and none can afford to lose.


## Episode 51: The Jingzhou Debt

In Sun Quan's camp, Lu Su and Sun Quan discuss how to reclaim Jingzhou from Liu Bei. Reports arrive that Liu Bei is actively expanding his military, and Sun Quan immediately dispatches Lu Su to first visit the ailing Zhou Yu at Baling, then proceed to Jingzhou to formally demand its return. At the Wu army camp, Zhou Yu's illness has worsened, but his hatred of Zhuge Liang has only intensified. Lu Su's visit hardens Zhou Yu's resolve to recapture Jingzhou by force if necessary.

In Jingzhou, Lu Su presents his case for the return of the province before the memorial tablet of Liu Qi (Liu Biao's son, whose death removed Liu Bei's last pretext for holding the territory). Zhuge Liang engages Lu Su in a sharp debate over Jingzhou's rightful ownership, and the two finally reach a written agreement: Liu Bei will return Jingzhou to Eastern Wu once he has conquered the western province of Yi (Sichuan). Zhou Yu dismisses this agreement as worthless paper and refuses to abandon his plans for a military solution.

Days later, news arrives that Lady Gan, Liu Bei's first wife, has died. Zhou Yu seizes on this as an opportunity: he proposes using Sun Quan's younger sister, Lady Sun (Sun Shangxiang), as bait to lure Liu Bei to the Wu capital of Jianye, where he can be taken hostage and exchanged for Jingzhou.

**Historical significance:** The dispute over Jingzhou is one of the pivotal conflicts of the Three Kingdoms period. Historically, Liu Bei did "borrow" Jingzhou's southern commanderies after Red Cliffs (208 CE), and the question of when or whether he would return them became a chronic source of tension between the Sun-Liu alliance. The written agreement to return Jingzhou after taking Yi Province appears in the novel Romance of the Three Kingdoms but is not well attested in historical records. The death of Lady Gan is historical, and the marriage plot involving Sun Shangxiang is one of the novel's most famous storylines. In real history, the marriage was a genuine political alliance arranged by Sun Quan (not primarily a trap), though later tradition and the novel reframed it as Zhou Yu's scheme.


## Episode 52: The Marriage Proposal

Sun Quan sends an envoy to propose marriage, inviting Liu Bei to travel south to Jianye for the wedding, citing Lady Wu's (Wu Guotai's) advanced age as the reason Liu Bei must come in person. Liu Bei hesitates and faces strong opposition from his advisors and sworn brothers, but ultimately decides to accept. He genuinely hopes the marriage alliance will strengthen the Sun-Liu pact against Cao Cao. Before departing, Liu Bei transfers full military and political authority over Jingzhou to Zhuge Liang, which alarms Guan Yu and Zhang Fei, who fear Zhuge Liang might overstep his bounds.

Liu Bei addresses their concerns by granting Zhuge Liang a sealed order authorizing punishment of anyone who disobeys. Following Zhuge Liang's instructions, Liu Bei makes a lavish public display of betrothal gifts upon arriving in Wu territory, ensuring that the news of the marriage spreads far and wide. When Lady Wu learns of the arrangement through public gossip rather than from her own son, she is furious. The resulting confrontation between Lady Wu and Sun Quan creates a rift between Sun Quan and Zhou Yu, whose secret scheme has been exposed by the publicity.

**Historical significance:** The marriage between Liu Bei and Sun Shangxiang did occur historically around 209 CE. It was a political marriage meant to cement the alliance after Red Cliffs. The novel's version, where Zhuge Liang gives sealed stratagems (the famous "three brocade bags" or 锦囊妙计) to help Liu Bei navigate the trap, is fictional embellishment. The character of Wu Guotai (Lady Wu) is a composite figure in the novel. Historically, Sun Quan's mother Lady Wu had already died before these events. The episode dramatizes the fundamental tension in the Sun-Liu relationship: allies against Cao Cao, but competitors for the same territory.


## Episode 53: The Power Struggle in Wu

Since Sun Quan took power, he has always shown deference and respect to Zhou Yu, but this time Zhou Yu's unilateral decision-making regarding the marriage plot pushes Sun Quan past his limit. Zhou Yu, furious at being overruled, throws down his official seal and storms out. Lady Wu intervenes to mediate the crisis, and eventually Sun Quan personally visits Zhou Yu's residence to return his military tally and restore his authority. However, Zhou Yu is stripped of his concurrent position as Praising Army Colonel (赞军都尉).

Zhou Yu continues to advocate for a military campaign against Jingzhou and has a candid, heart-to-heart conversation with Lu Su about his strategic vision. Meanwhile, Lady Wu decides to turn the sham marriage into a real one, genuinely intending to wed her daughter to Liu Bei. Zhou Yu, adapting to this new reality, plants armed soldiers at the Ganlu Temple (Sweet Dew Temple): if Sun Shangxiang does not choose Liu Bei at the meeting, the assassins will strike.

**Historical significance:** This episode dramatizes the internal politics of Eastern Wu, particularly the tension between Sun Quan as sovereign and Zhou Yu as his most powerful military commander. Historically, Zhou Yu held enormous independent authority, and the power dynamic between them was more collaborative than adversarial. The Ganlu Temple ambush is a novelistic invention, though it draws on the real political complexity surrounding the marriage. The episode illustrates how the show portrays Zhou Yu as increasingly desperate and irrational in contrast to his historical reputation as a brilliant, magnanimous commander. The historical Zhou Yu was described by contemporaries as generous and broad-minded, quite unlike the jealous schemer of the novel.


## Episode 54: The Ganlu Temple

In Sun Shangxiang's chambers, Lady Wu tells her daughter that if she approves of Liu Bei, she should play the jade flute left behind by her father Sun Jian as a signal. At the Ganlu Temple, Lu Meng has hidden armed men, waiting for Liu Bei's arrival. Despite Zhao Yun's protests, Liu Bei insists on entering the temple with only a small personal guard. Lady Wu is immediately taken with Liu Bei's dignified bearing and noble demeanor.

Zhao Yun publicly exposes Lu Meng's hidden ambush, throwing the proceedings into chaos. Sun Quan moves to execute Lu Meng for the embarrassment, but Liu Bei magnanimously intercedes to save him. Liu Bei then delivers an impassioned speech about his lifelong dedication to restoring the Han dynasty, moving everyone present. At the crucial moment, the sound of Sun Shangxiang's flute drifts down from her chambers, signaling her approval. The wedding proceeds. On the wedding night, Liu Bei finds Sun Shangxiang's chambers lined with weapons and armed maids. She challenges him to a martial contest before accepting him as her husband.

Zhou Yu visits Lady Wu and persuades her to keep Liu Bei and his new wife in Jianye for an extended stay. Liu Bei sees through this as Zhou Yu's ploy to keep him away from Jingzhou, but deliberately plays along, reasoning that the longer he stays in Wu, the more time Zhuge Liang has to consolidate Jingzhou's defenses. Zhou Yu decides to use a life of luxury and indulgence to weaken Liu Bei's will, and Liu Bei cooperates fully, neglecting even his riding and archery practice.

**Historical significance:** The Ganlu Temple scene is one of the most theatrical set pieces in the Romance of the Three Kingdoms and is entirely fictional. Sun Shangxiang's portrayal as a martial woman who keeps weapons in her chambers does have some basis in the historical records: the Sanguozhi notes that she had over a hundred armed female attendants and that Liu Bei felt uneasy around her. The flute signal, the ambush, and Liu Bei's speech are novelistic inventions designed to showcase Liu Bei's charisma and moral authority. The "honey trap" strategy of softening Liu Bei with luxury reflects a real concern: the novel's point is that even a hero can be dulled by comfort, a theme that recurs throughout the Three Kingdoms narrative.


## Episode 55: The Escape from Wu

As the year draws to a close, Liu Bei has grown visibly soft and complacent in his Eastern Wu residence, indulging in luxury and demanding ever-larger monthly stipends from Sun Quan. Zhou Yu is delighted by this decline. Zhao Yun, unable to bear watching his lord's deterioration, publicly confronts a drunken Liu Bei, and the two quarrel bitterly. Liu Bei angrily orders Zhao Yun to return to Jingzhou.

Back in Jingzhou, Zhuge Liang punishes Zhao Yun with thirty strokes of the military rod, then immediately orders him to return to Jiangdong to protect Liu Bei. Guan Yu and Zhang Fei advocate a surprise attack on Jianye to rescue their brother, but Zhuge Liang refuses. Zhou Yu dispatches Lu Meng to spread rumors in Jingzhou that Zhuge Liang has made a secret pact with Zhou Yu and intends to seize power for himself.

In his Wu residence, Liu Bei and Zhao Yun secretly discuss plans to return to Jingzhou. Liu Bei decides to use a fabricated report of Cao Cao threatening Jingzhou as his pretext for leaving. Sun Shangxiang senses the deception but helps by requesting Lady Wu's permission to leave the city under the cover of visiting Liu Bei's ancestral graves. In Jingzhou, Zhang Fei drunkenly rampages, berating Zhuge Liang for failing to rescue Liu Bei, and overturns Zhuge Liang's command desk. Zhuge Liang endures the humiliation for the sake of the greater cause. At a banquet, Lady Wu deliberately gets Sun Quan drunk, allowing Liu Bei's party to slip out of the city. But Zhou Yu has already set up an ambush along the escape route.

**Historical significance:** The "escape from Wu" sequence draws loosely on real events. Historically, Liu Bei did eventually leave Wu territory with Sun Shangxiang, but the circumstances were less dramatic than the novel depicts. The quarrel between Zhao Yun and Liu Bei, the thirty strokes punishment, and the elaborate escape plan are fictional additions. Zhang Fei's insubordination toward Zhuge Liang reflects a recurring novelistic theme about the tension between Liu Bei's sworn brotherhood and his reliance on his chief strategist. The rumor campaign is the novel's way of showing Zhou Yu as a master of psychological warfare, though historically there is little evidence of such specific operations.


## Episode 56: Zhou Yu Vomits Blood

At the moment of crisis, Zhao Yun opens the third of Zhuge Liang's sealed stratagem bags. The plan: have Sun Shangxiang personally confront the pursuing Wu generals. When she steps forward and demands passage, the Wu officers Xu Sheng and Ding Feng, unwilling to harm their lord's sister, have no choice but to let Liu Bei's party through.

Meanwhile in Jingzhou, another crisis is brewing. Zhang Fei's erratic behavior has alarmed Zhuge Liang, who suspects Zhang Fei is planning a mutiny (兵谏, a "military remonstrance") to force a rescue mission. Ma Su suggests bringing in local Jingzhou troops to counter Zhang Fei, but Zhuge Liang does the opposite: he moves all Jingzhou garrison troops out of the city. At the riverbank, Zhou Yu personally leads soldiers to intercept Liu Bei. But Huang Zhong and Wei Yan, positioned in advance by Zhuge Liang, spring their ambush and rout the Wu forces. Liu Bei boards his ship safely. Zhou Yu, overwhelmed with rage and frustration, vomits blood.

Back in Gong'an, Zhang Fei and Guan Yu have surrounded the government offices, trying to force Zhuge Liang's hand. Liu Bei's timely return defuses the standoff. Zhuge Liang, deeply hurt by the brothers' distrust, hangs up his seal of office and departs. The three sworn brothers ride after him to apologize. In Jiangdong, Zhou Yu lies in his tent coughing blood, his body failing but his mind still scheming. He lays out a four-step plan to retake Jingzhou, and Lu Su pledges his support.

**Historical significance:** The scene of Zhou Yu vomiting blood after being outwitted by Zhuge Liang is the novel's dramatization of the "Three Humiliations of Zhou Yu" (三气周瑜), one of the most famous sequences in Chinese literature. None of this is historical. The real Zhou Yu died in 210 CE, likely from illness contracted during the Red Cliffs campaign, not from rage at Zhuge Liang. The sealed stratagem bags (锦囊妙计) have become a Chinese idiom meaning a brilliant pre-planned solution to a future crisis. Sun Shangxiang confronting Wu soldiers is novelistic but captures a real dynamic: her position between two rival states made her a uniquely powerful figure. Huang Zhong and Wei Yan's appearance here foreshadows their growing importance in Liu Bei's campaigns.


## Episode 57: The Death of Zhou Yu

In Jingzhou, Lu Su arrives on orders to demand the return of Jiangxia and Nanjun. Zhuge Liang deftly deflects the demand, and Lu Su shifts to discussing Liu Bei's promised campaign against Yi Province (western Sichuan) as the precondition for returning Jingzhou. Zhuge Liang predictably cites insufficient troops and supplies as reasons for delay. Lu Su then springs Zhou Yu's real plan: the "Borrow a Road to Destroy Guo" (假途灭虢) stratagem. Zhou Yu proposes that the Wu army march through Jingzhou to attack Yi Province on Liu Bei's behalf, but the true intent is to seize Jingzhou as the army passes through.

Liu Bei panics upon receiving Zhou Yu's personal letter proposing this plan, fearing Yi Province will fall to Wu. But Zhuge Liang sees through the ruse immediately. He mobilizes all available forces and uses the terrain advantage to set an ambush. When Zhou Yu leads his army against Jingzhou, he walks straight into Zhuge Liang's trap. The Wu forces suffer a devastating defeat. Zhou Yu's body, already weakened by illness and rage, collapses further.

Zhou Yu orders a retreat to preserve what remains of his army. Back in Jiangdong, Zhou Yu is on his deathbed. Sun Quan rushes to his side. With his final breaths, Zhou Yu counsels Sun Quan to bide his time and pursue the long game for Eastern Wu's survival. He recommends Lu Su as his successor as Grand Commander. Then Zhou Yu looks to the heavens and utters his famous dying words: "If Heaven gave birth to Yu, why did it also give birth to Liang?" (既生瑜，何生亮). He dies.

**Historical significance:** Zhou Yu's death in 210 CE is historical, but the circumstances are entirely different from the novel. The real Zhou Yu died while preparing a campaign to take Yi Province (Sichuan), likely from illness. He was 35 years old. His dying recommendation of Lu Su as successor is attested in historical sources. The famous line "既生瑜，何生亮" is a novelistic invention and has become one of the most quoted phrases in Chinese culture, used to express the frustration of being overshadowed by a contemporary rival. Historically, Zhou Yu and Zhuge Liang had minimal direct interaction, and Zhou Yu's stature as a commander was at least equal to Zhuge Liang's. The "Borrow a Road to Destroy Guo" plan references the ancient stratagem from the Spring and Autumn period (the fall of the state of Guo), showing how the novel layers classical allusions into its plot. Zhou Yu's death marks the end of an era for Eastern Wu and shifts the Sun-Liu dynamic permanently.


## Episode 58: The Phoenix Chick at Leiyang

Sun Quan informs Lu Su of Zhou Yu's dying wish to make him Grand Commander. Even Lu Meng endorses the appointment, surprising Lu Su. At Zhou Yu's grand funeral, Zhuge Liang arrives alone to pay his respects, delivering an emotional and eloquent eulogy that moves the assembled Wu generals to tears. But the mourning is interrupted by an ugly, disheveled stranger named Pang Tong, who loudly claims that it was his Chain Link Stratagem (连环计), not Zhuge Liang's brilliance, that truly won the Battle of Red Cliffs. Lu Su has him thrown out.

Afterward, Zhuge Liang seeks out Pang Tong and tries to persuade him to serve Liu Bei, but Pang Tong refuses. He later travels to Jingzhou on his own, using the alias "Long Guang" to apply for a position. Liu Bei, judging by appearances and put off by Pang Tong's homely looks, assigns him the lowly post of county magistrate of Leiyang. Pang Tong arrives at Leiyang and proceeds to completely neglect his duties. Liu Bei sends Zhang Fei and Sun Qian to investigate. In a stunning display, Pang Tong sits down before Zhang Fei and clears through one hundred days' worth of accumulated cases and paperwork in a single session, leaving Zhang Fei astonished. Zhang Fei races back to report what he has witnessed, and Liu Bei personally rides through the night to Leiyang to recruit this extraordinary talent.

**Historical significance:** Pang Tong (styled Shiyuan, nicknamed "the Phoenix Chick" or 凤雏) is one of the great figures of the Three Kingdoms. His reputation was paired with Zhuge Liang's: the saying went "Sleeping Dragon and Phoenix Chick, obtain either one and you can pacify the realm" (卧龙凤雏，得一可安天下). The Leiyang episode, where Pang Tong clears a hundred days of legal cases in a morning, appears in the novel and is one of the most celebrated demonstrations of genius in Chinese storytelling, though its historicity is uncertain. The claim about the Chain Link Stratagem at Red Cliffs is the novel's invention, connecting Pang Tong to the fire attack strategy. Historically, Pang Tong served Zhou Yu first and was recommended to Liu Bei by both Zhuge Liang and Lu Su after Zhou Yu's death. His initial poor reception by Liu Bei due to his appearance is attested in historical sources and serves as a commentary on the danger of judging talent by outward appearance.


## Episode 59: The Bronze Sparrow Terrace

In Leiyang, Liu Bei personally serves wine for Pang Tong and repeatedly tries to persuade him to stay. Though Pang Tong initially resists, Liu Bei wins him over by gifting him his own horse, the famous Dilu (的卢). Moved by this generosity, Pang Tong agrees to return to Jingzhou and serve Liu Bei.

The story then shifts to Cao Cao's realm. In the fifteenth year of the Jian'an era (210 CE), Cao Cao completes the construction of the magnificent Bronze Sparrow Terrace (铜雀台) and hosts a grand banquet. His sons Cao Zhang and Cao Zhi attend, with Cao Pi warmly receiving his brothers. During the festivities, a drunken Cao Zhi insults and ejects Sima Yi from his quarters. Cao Pi personally escorts Sima Yi out and attempts to win him over as an advisor.

The Terrace gathering is revealed to be Cao Cao's method of testing his sons to determine a successor. He assigns them the topic "Bronze Sparrow Terrace" and orders each to compose a fu (rhapsody). Cao Zhi's composition is brilliant, earning universal praise. Cao Pi's effort appears inferior by comparison. However, Sima Yi offers a shrewd analysis: Cao Zhi's dazzling literary display won him only a fief but alienated the conservative Han court officials, while Cao Pi's more restrained, respectful fu won the hearts of scholars across the realm. The succession contest has begun, and Sima Yi has placed his bet on Cao Pi.

**Historical significance:** The Bronze Sparrow Terrace (铜雀台) was a real structure completed by Cao Cao in 210 CE in Ye (modern Hebei). The literary contest between Cao Pi and Cao Zhi is one of the great stories of the era and has significant historical basis. Both brothers were genuinely talented poets, and their rivalry shaped Cao Wei politics for a generation. Cao Zhi's "Ode to the Bronze Sparrow Terrace" (登台赋) is a famous work of Chinese literature. The succession struggle between Cao Pi and Cao Zhi is well documented in historical sources. Sima Yi's role as Cao Pi's key advisor is historical and proved decisive: Sima Yi helped Cao Pi navigate court politics to eventually become Cao Cao's heir. The show's portrayal of Sima Yi as a calculating strategist who chose Cao Pi early reflects both the novel's characterization and the historical record. This subplot plants seeds for events decades later, when the Sima family would ultimately overthrow the Cao Wei dynasty.


## Episode 60: Ma Teng Enters the Capital

Cao Cao wants Sima Yi to serve as Cao Zhi's advisor, but Sima Yi refuses. Cao Cao, enraged, expels him from the chancellor's residence. Cao Pi, deeply moved by Sima Yi's loyalty, chases him outside the city to invite him to his own household. Sima Yi insists on living in seclusion outside the city walls, maintaining secret contact with Cao Pi from a distance.

During the Mid-Autumn Festival, Ma Teng of Xiliang (the western frontier) sends Cao Cao a gift of cream cheese as a veiled insult. Cao Cao decides to adopt Xun Yu's plan: issue an imperial edict in the Emperor's name ordering Ma Teng to bring his army east to attack Sun Quan, then detain Ma Teng and his sons when they pass through the capital Xuchang. Ma Teng suspects this is a trap and decides to turn it against Cao Cao. Ma Teng and his ally Han Sui stage a fake falling-out to deceive Cao Cao's intelligence, then secretly plan to assassinate Cao Cao and seize Xuchang. Cao Cao sends his son Cao Pi to welcome Ma Teng outside the city, and at the welcome banquet, Cao Cao treats Ma Teng with elaborate courtesy. The next day, Cao Cao dispatches Cao Zhang to inspect Ma Teng's camp. Inside the camp, Ma Teng reveals his assassination plan to the official Huang Kui, who enthusiastically agrees to help and decides to recruit his brother-in-law Miao Ze as an inside agent.

**Historical significance:** The Ma Teng storyline draws on historical events but rearranges the timeline significantly. In real history, Ma Teng traveled to the capital Ye (not Xuchang) around 208 CE as a gesture of submission to Cao Cao, and his sons were kept as hostages. It was Ma Teng's son Ma Chao who later rebelled against Cao Cao in 211 CE (the Battle of Tong Pass), and Cao Cao executed Ma Teng in retaliation. The novel reverses the causation: it depicts Ma Teng plotting against Cao Cao first, with Cao Cao executing him in self-defense, then Ma Chao seeking revenge. This rearrangement makes Ma Teng a martyr and Ma Chao's rebellion a filial duty rather than an opportunistic power grab. The Sima Yi subplot in this episode continues to build the show's long-game narrative. Historically, Sima Yi was indeed slow to accept service under Cao Cao and was closely aligned with Cao Pi, laying the groundwork for the Sima clan's eventual dominance.


*Episodes 51-60 span roughly 209 to 211 CE in the historical timeline, covering the transition from the post-Red Cliffs settlement to the opening of new fronts in the west. The dramatic center is the death of Zhou Yu and the permanent fracture of the Sun-Liu alliance over Jingzhou, a wound that will eventually prove fatal to both kingdoms' ambitions against Cao Wei.*
