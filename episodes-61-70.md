# New Three Kingdoms (新三国) - Episodes 61-70
*From Ambition to Ashes: Cao Cao's Rise to King, the Fall of Shu's Phoenix, and the Road to Hanzhong*

---

## Episode 61: The Trap at the Capital

Cao Zhi holds a celebratory banquet at his residence after his triumph at the Bronze Sparrow Terrace, where he has been rewarded with a noble title. His friend Yang Xiu astutely points out that Cao Cao granted the title not to honor Cao Zhi's literary talent but to reward his essay urging Cao Cao to accept imperial elevation. Cao Pi's essay, by contrast, contained language deferential to the Han dynasty, which displeased their father. Sima Yi visits Cao Pi and counsels patience, observing that the absence of key officials like the Xun clan from Cao Zhi's banquet proves that the loyal old guard still favors the Han court. Cao Pi confesses he had actually written a second essay urging his father's elevation, but held it back when he saw that the loyalist ministers stayed away. From this point on, Cao Pi and Sima Yi forge a close political alliance.

Meanwhile, the loyalist official Huang Kui approaches Miao Ze with a plan to open the northern gate and admit Ma Teng's troops into the capital to assassinate Cao Cao. Miao Ze weighs his options and ultimately betrays the conspiracy to Cao Cao, who lays an overnight ambush. Ma Teng's forces charge into the city and are caught in the trap. Ma Teng fights bravely but is captured and executed. When Huang Kui's confession implicates Cao Pi, Cao Cao orders his son seized. Cao Pi flatly denies everything and deflects blame onto Cao Zhi, narrowly escaping punishment.

**Historical significance:** Ma Teng's death in the capital is drawn from the novel's dramatization of events around 211 CE. Historically, Ma Teng had been summoned to the capital years earlier and was indeed executed by Cao Cao, but the circumstances were less theatrical. The show compresses and dramatizes the Ma Teng conspiracy to set up the succession rivalry between Cao Pi and Cao Zhi, which was a genuine and consequential political struggle in the state of Wei. The Bronze Sparrow Terrace poetry contest is a famous set piece from the novel, not recorded in the histories.

## Episode 62: Xu Chu Fights Ma Chao

News of Ma Teng's execution reaches his son Ma Chao in the northwest. Consumed by vengeance, Ma Chao rallies the Xiliang (Western Liang) forces and marches on Cao Cao. The campaign opens disastrously for Cao Cao: his army is routed, and he is forced to cut off his beard and discard his robe to escape Ma Chao's pursuit. Cao Hong volunteers to lead a flanking force behind Ma Chao's lines. When Ma Chao presses his attack to the banks of the Wei River, the fierce bodyguard Xu Chu strips off his armor and fights Ma Chao bare-chested in one of the series' most celebrated duels, buying time for Cao Cao's forces to regroup. Cao Hong's cavalry arrives and smashes the Xiliang army.

In the aftermath, Han Sui, Ma Chao's uneasy ally, proposes a truce with Cao Cao. When Han Sui meets Cao Cao at the front lines for a long, seemingly friendly conversation about old times in the capital, Ma Chao grows deeply suspicious. Cao Cao then executes his famous stratagem: he sends Han Sui a letter with key passages deliberately smudged and altered. Ma Chao intercepts the letter and concludes that Han Sui has been secretly colluding with Cao Cao. The resulting distrust between the two Xiliang leaders erupts into open fighting within their own camp. Cao Cao seizes the moment to crush the divided enemy. Ma Chao flees west in defeat, eventually taking refuge with the warlord Zhang Lu in Hanzhong.

**Historical significance:** The Battle of Tong Pass (211 CE) is one of the most vivid military episodes in Three Kingdoms history. Xu Chu's naked duel with Ma Chao is embellished from the novel but rooted in Xu Chu's historical reputation as a fearsome warrior. The stratagem of the doctored letter to drive a wedge between Ma Chao and Han Sui is recorded in the historical text "Records of the Three Kingdoms" (Serta Sanguo Zhi) and is considered one of Cao Cao's finest applications of psychological warfare. Ma Chao's defeat here set the stage for his later defection to Liu Bei.

## Episode 63: Zhang Song Offers a Map

The focus shifts to the southwest. Zhang Song, a counselor serving the weak lord Liu Zhang in Yi Province (Sichuan), travels to Cao Cao's court hoping to form an alliance. Cao Cao, arrogant and dismissive, humiliates the physically unimpressive Zhang Song. Furious at this treatment, Zhang Song departs empty-handed. On his return journey he encounters Liu Bei, who treats him with warmth and genuine respect. Moved by this contrast, Zhang Song secretly presents Liu Bei with a detailed military map of Yi Province and urges him to take the territory from Liu Zhang.

Back in Yi Province, Zhang Song and his ally Fa Zheng begin laying the groundwork for Liu Bei's entry into the region. They convince Liu Zhang that an alliance with Liu Bei is the best defense against Zhang Lu's forces threatening from Hanzhong to the north. Liu Zhang, persuaded by their arguments, sends an invitation for Liu Bei to bring his army into Yi Province. Liu Bei hesitates, torn between ambition and his commitment to righteousness, since Liu Zhang is a fellow member of the Liu imperial clan.

**Historical significance:** Zhang Song's mission to Cao Cao and subsequent defection to Liu Bei is based on historical events around 211-212 CE. The map of Yi Province is a famous detail from the novel. Historically, Fa Zheng and Zhang Song did conspire to invite Liu Bei into Yi Province, and Liu Zhang did welcome Liu Bei as an ally against Zhang Lu. This episode marks the beginning of Liu Bei's campaign to take the Sichuan basin, which would become the territorial foundation of his Shu Han kingdom.

---

## Episode 64: The Banquet at Fucheng

Liu Bei leads his forces into Yi Province at Liu Zhang's invitation. The two Liu clan lords meet at Fucheng for a great welcoming banquet. Liu Bei's advisor Fa Zheng privately urges him to seize Liu Zhang at the feast, but Liu Bei refuses, unwilling to break faith with a kinsman. Pang Tong, Liu Bei's chief strategist, is less restrained. He secretly arranges for the general Wei Yan to perform a sword dance during the banquet as cover for an assassination attempt on Liu Zhang. Wei Yan draws his blade in the middle of the feast, sparking a chaotic melee between soldiers of both sides. Liu Bei intervenes and stops the fighting, declaring to Zhang Ren (Liu Zhang's loyal general) that he will never commit such a dishonorable act.

When Zhang Lu's forces attack Jiameng Pass to the north, Zhang Ren suggests that Liu Zhang order Liu Bei to march out and repel the invaders as a test of his sincerity. Liu Bei accepts the assignment and successfully drives back Zhang Lu's troops, which temporarily reassures Liu Zhang. Meanwhile in the east, Sun Quan's generals urge him to seize Jing Province while Liu Bei is occupied in the west. Sun Quan refuses, but the ailing Lu Su agrees to Sun Quan's plan to promote Lu Meng as deputy commander, signaling that Wu is quietly preparing for the eventual contest over Jing Province.

**Historical significance:** The banquet at Fucheng is based on historical and novelistic accounts of Liu Bei's entry into Yi Province (212 CE). The sword dance assassination attempt is a dramatic invention echoing the famous Hongmen Banquet of early Chinese history. Historically, Liu Bei did spend roughly a year cooperating with Liu Zhang before hostilities broke out. The show uses this episode to highlight Liu Bei's central moral dilemma: his ambition for territory versus his self-image as a righteous leader. Lu Meng's quiet rise in Wu foreshadows his later, devastating capture of Jing Province from Guan Yu.

---

## Episode 65: The Plot Unravels

Tensions between Liu Bei and Liu Zhang continue to build. Liu Bei remains reluctant to move against his host, but Pang Tong devises a two-pronged strategy: he arranges for a forged letter to be delivered that frames Zhang Song as plotting to betray Liu Zhang to Liu Bei. The deception works perfectly. Liu Zhang, enraged at the apparent treachery, has Zhang Song arrested and executed. With Zhang Song dead, Liu Zhang turns hostile and mobilizes all of Yi Province's forces against Liu Bei.

Liu Bei, who genuinely valued Zhang Song, is devastated. Yet Pang Tong points out that the situation is now clarified: Liu Zhang has broken the peace and attacked first, giving Liu Bei moral justification to fight back. The two camps begin maneuvering for open war across the hills and valleys of western Sichuan. Pang Tong presses Liu Bei to strike quickly and march east toward the strategic city of Luocheng before Liu Zhang's forces can consolidate. Liu Bei, pushed by circumstance beyond his scruples, agrees. The stage is set for the decisive battle for Yi Province.

**Historical significance:** Zhang Song's execution is recorded in the historical sources. In both the novel and the histories, Zhang Song's conspiracy was discovered when his brother Zhang Su reported it to Liu Zhang. The show's version, which makes Pang Tong the architect of the exposure, heightens the moral complexity: Liu Bei's own side engineered the provocation that forced the war. This reflects a broader pattern in Three Kingdoms storytelling where Liu Bei's famous benevolence is shown to depend on strategists willing to do what he will not.

## Episode 66: The Phoenix Falls at Fallen Phoenix Slope

Liu Bei's army advances toward Luocheng, but the road is treacherous. Pang Tong, whose courtesy name "Fengchu" means "Young Phoenix," recognizes that the narrow valley called Fallen Phoenix Slope (Luofeng Po) is a perfect site for an ambush. Rather than avoid it, Pang Tong makes a fateful decision: he borrows Liu Bei's distinctive horse, the Dilu ("Leaping Steed"), and insists on leading the column through the pass himself. Zhang Ren's archers lie in wait. When they see the famous horse, they assume its rider is Liu Bei and unleash a storm of arrows. Pang Tong is struck dozens of times and falls from the saddle, dying in the valley. He has left behind a letter for Liu Bei explaining that he chose this death deliberately: by sacrificing himself, he has given Liu Bei the righteous cause ("Liu Zhang murdered my advisor") needed to wage all-out war for Yi Province.

Liu Bei is overcome with grief and rage. He swears to take Yi Province to honor Pang Tong's sacrifice. In Jing Province, Zhuge Liang receives word of Pang Tong's death and decides he must personally lead reinforcements into the west. Before departing, Zhuge Liang entrusts the great seal of Jing Province to Guan Yu with a crucial admonition: "Resist Cao Cao to the north, maintain the alliance with Sun Quan to the east." In Chengdu, Liu Zhang is alarmed by Liu Bei's advance. His advisor Huang Quan suggests ceding territory to Zhang Lu in exchange for military support. Zhang Lu eagerly agrees and dispatches Ma Chao with thirty thousand elite troops to attack Jiameng Pass. Zhang Fei volunteers to meet Ma Chao in battle, and the two legendary warriors clash from daybreak until nightfall in one of the great duels of the series.

**Historical significance:** Pang Tong's death at Fallen Phoenix Slope in 214 CE is one of the most famous tragedies in Three Kingdoms lore. Historically, Pang Tong was killed by a stray arrow during the siege of Luocheng, and the name "Fallen Phoenix Slope" was likely attached to the site retroactively. The show's version, where Pang Tong engineers his own martyrdom, is pure dramatic invention but captures the novel's theme of loyalty unto death. Zhuge Liang's transfer of Jing Province to Guan Yu is a pivotal historical moment: the eight-word instruction ("north resist Cao Cao, east ally Sun Quan") would become tragically ironic when Guan Yu violated the second half. Zhang Fei versus Ma Chao is a celebrated fictional duel from the novel.

## Episode 67: Ma Chao Pledges Allegiance

The duel between Zhang Fei and Ma Chao rages on without a clear victor. Liu Bei, watching from a distance, recognizes Ma Chao as an extraordinary warrior and wants him on his side. Zhuge Liang, having arrived in Yi Province, devises a scheme to turn Ma Chao away from Zhang Lu. He sends agents to bribe Zhang Lu's advisor Yang Song, who whispers slander about Ma Chao's loyalty into Zhang Lu's ear. Zhang Lu takes the bait and orders Ma Chao to withdraw from the front lines under the pretext of a Cao Cao invasion. When Ma Chao refuses the recall order, Zhang Lu is furious and sets impossible conditions for his return. Ma Chao, trapped between an ungrateful master and a hostile enemy, is left stranded outside the walls of Hanzhong.

Zhuge Liang personally visits Ma Chao's camp and persuades him to join Liu Bei. Ma Chao swears allegiance. His defection changes the balance of power immediately: when Ma Chao leads his fearsome cavalry against Chengdu, Liu Zhang is terrified. Resistance crumbles. Liu Zhang opens the gates and surrenders the city. In Yi Province's government hall, Liu Bei sincerely invites the loyalist minister Huang Quan to serve the cause of restoring the Han dynasty. Huang Quan, moved to tears, accepts. Yi Province now belongs to Liu Bei, who governs it with a firm hand. Zhuge Liang observes privately that Liu Bei has transformed: the wandering exile has become a sovereign.

**Historical significance:** Ma Chao's defection to Liu Bei in 214 CE was a real and significant event. Historically, Ma Chao had been mistreated by Zhang Lu and approached Liu Bei on his own initiative; the novel and show add Zhuge Liang's machinations for dramatic effect. Liu Zhang's surrender of Chengdu is historical and marks the founding of Liu Bei's territorial base. The show's portrayal of Liu Bei's transformation from righteous wanderer to calculating ruler reflects the historical shift as Liu Bei assumed the mantle of a regional power. Huang Quan was indeed a notable Yi Province loyalist who eventually served both Shu Han and, after his forced defection, Wei.

## Episode 68: Guan Yu Attends a Feast Armed with Only a Blade

With Liu Bei now master of Yi Province, the question of Jing Province becomes urgent. Sun Quan and Lu Su discuss how to reclaim the three commanderies that Liu Bei had borrowed. Sun Quan sends Zhuge Liang's brother, Zhuge Jin, to Chengdu to request the return of the territory, but Guan Yu, who holds Jing Province, flatly refuses to hand over even a single commandery and drives away Wu's appointed officials by force.

Lu Su, gravely ill, makes one final personal effort. He invites Guan Yu to a banquet to negotiate. Guan Yu arrives alone, armed with nothing but his Green Dragon Crescent Blade, in the famous episode known as "attending the feast with a single blade" (dan dao fu hui). At the banquet, Lu Su and Guan Yu engage in a fierce verbal duel over the rightful ownership of Jing Province. At the critical moment, Guan Yu, moved by Lu Su's sincerity and integrity, agrees to return the three southern commanderies of Changsha, Guiyang, and Lingling to Wu. Lu Su, his mission accomplished but his health broken, passes away soon after. On his deathbed, he implores Lu Meng to maintain the alliance with Liu Bei against Cao Cao as the foundational strategy of Wu.

In Xuchang, a different crisis is unfolding. Court officials petition Emperor Xian to grant Cao Cao the title of King of Wei. Only the venerable Xun Yu stands in opposition, arguing passionately against the elevation. The emperor, powerless, grants the title anyway. Outside the city gates, Cao Zhi rides recklessly through the restricted White Horse Gate, violating imperial protocol. Xun Yu confronts him with a furious rebuke, but the incident only deepens the growing rift between the old loyalist guard and Cao Cao's increasingly imperial household.

**Historical significance:** The "Single Blade Feast" (dan dao fu hui, ~215 CE) is one of Guan Yu's most famous episodes. Historically, it was actually Lu Su who invited Guan Yu and aggressively pressed the case for returning Jing Province, with Lu Su being the assertive party rather than the gentle diplomat of the novel. Lu Su's death in 217 CE was a turning point: his policy of alliance with Liu Bei died with him, and Lu Meng would pursue a far more aggressive approach. Cao Cao's elevation to King of Wei in 216 CE was a watershed moment signaling that the Han dynasty existed in name only. Xun Yu's opposition is placed slightly earlier in the show for dramatic effect but reflects his genuine historical stance against Cao Cao's imperial pretensions.

## Episode 69: The Empty Box

Cao Cao, now King of Wei in all but name, pushes further to consolidate his authority. When he attempts to tear down the White Horse Gate as a symbol of the old imperial order, Xun Yu is outraged. The man who spent decades as Cao Cao's most trusted strategist can no longer reconcile his loyalty to his lord with his loyalty to the Han dynasty. Xun Yu falls ill, consumed by the impossible contradiction of his position. Cao Cao sends his advisor Cheng Yu to visit the sick man and delivers what appears to be a gift: a box of fine fruits. Xun Yu opens the box and finds it empty. He understands the message immediately: there is nothing left for you here. Xun Yu laughs, then takes his own life.

At Xun Yu's funeral, Cao Cao and Cao Pi attend in person, displaying extravagant grief. But the political machinery does not pause. Pro-Han loyalists in the capital, led by the official Geng Ji, plot a coup against Cao Cao's regime. The conspiracy is discovered, and it falls to Cao Pi to suppress the rebellion. He does so with decisive brutality, proving his capability as a political and military leader. Cao Cao, observing his son's performance, begins to tilt the succession in Cao Pi's favor over the more literary Cao Zhi. The episode closes with Cao Cao's attention turning south toward the strategic region of Hanzhong, where Liu Bei's growing power threatens to upset the balance between the three kingdoms.

**Historical significance:** Xun Yu's death in 212 CE is one of the most poignant episodes in Three Kingdoms history. The empty box is a famous detail from the novel (in some versions, the box contained food but was empty, symbolizing "there is no more Han to serve"). Historically, Xun Yu died of illness shortly after opposing Cao Cao's elevation to Duke of Wei, and many historians believe he was driven to suicide or poisoned. Xun Yu's tragedy embodies the impossible position of those who served Cao Cao believing they were restoring order to the Han empire, only to realize they were building its replacement. Geng Ji's rebellion (218 CE) is a real historical event; placing it here allows the show to showcase Cao Pi's political competence. The succession struggle between Cao Pi and Cao Zhi was one of the defining dramas of Wei's early years.

## Episode 70: The Death of Yang Xiu

The Hanzhong campaign reaches its climax. Huang Zhong leads Liu Bei's forces in an assault on Mount Dingjun, the key stronghold defending Hanzhong. The garrison commander Xiahou Yuan, one of Cao Cao's most capable generals, fights a desperate defense and sends urgent pleas for reinforcement. Cao Cao dispatches a relief column, but it arrives too late. Huang Zhong, the aging warrior, leads a charge that kills Xiahou Yuan on the mountain, securing Mount Dingjun for Liu Bei and breaking Cao Cao's hold on Hanzhong.

Liu Bei presses his advantage, committing his entire army to the Hanzhong theater. Cao Cao's forces are drawn into a grinding war of attrition. Frustrated and suffering from his worsening headaches, Cao Cao issues the cryptic nighttime password "chicken ribs" (ji lei). Yang Xiu, the brilliant but reckless aide, instantly deciphers the meaning: chicken ribs are tasteless to eat but a waste to throw away, just as Hanzhong has become for Cao Cao. Yang Xiu tells his comrades to pack their belongings for a retreat to Xuchang. When Cao Cao discovers that Yang Xiu has broadcast his private indecision to the entire camp, he executes him for sowing disorder. The real offense, however, is deeper: Yang Xiu had long supported Cao Zhi in the succession struggle, and his death is also a political purge.

The campaign turns decisively against Cao Cao. His headaches worsen, and Sima Yi, ever the pragmatist, orders the withdrawal to Xuchang. Liu Bei's followers urge him to proclaim himself King of Hanzhong, and he does so, reaching the pinnacle of his power. But Zhuge Liang watches the celebrations with deep unease, sensing that overconfidence will lead to disaster. In Jing Province, Guan Yu is emboldened by Liu Bei's victories and resolves to attack the Cao Wei fortress at Fan Castle, hoping to coordinate a grand north-south offensive. His decision to march north will have catastrophic consequences.

**Historical significance:** The Battle of Mount Dingjun (219 CE) and Xiahou Yuan's death are among the most important military events of the Three Kingdoms period. Huang Zhong's slaying of Xiahou Yuan is historical, and it was the decisive moment that gave Liu Bei control of Hanzhong. Yang Xiu's execution over the "chicken ribs" incident is one of the most famous anecdotes in Chinese literature, illustrating both Cao Cao's ruthlessness and the danger of being too clever at court. Historically, Yang Xiu's death was tied to his involvement in the Cao Zhi faction. Liu Bei's self-proclamation as King of Hanzhong in 219 CE marked the high-water mark of his career. Guan Yu's subsequent attack on Fan Castle, while initially successful, would lead to the loss of Jing Province and his own death, fundamentally altering the trajectory of the Three Kingdoms era.
