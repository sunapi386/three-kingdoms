## Episode 3: The Murder at Lu Boshe's Estate (曹操误杀吕伯奢)

Cao Cao and Chen Gong are now wanted fugitives, their faces posted on notices throughout the region. As night falls, they seek shelter at a remote estate belonging to Lu Boshe, an old sworn brother of Cao Cao's father. The elderly Lu warmly welcomes the pair and insists on riding out to buy wine to properly entertain his honored guests.

After Lu Boshe departs, Cao Cao and Chen Gong hear the sounds of blades being sharpened and servants shouting in the courtyard. Cao Cao, paranoid and on edge, concludes that Lu Boshe has gone to alert the authorities and that the servants are preparing to kill them. He and Chen Gong burst out and slaughter everyone in the household, only to discover that the servants had been preparing to butcher a pig for dinner. It was hospitality, not treachery.

Horrified, the two flee into the night. On the road, they encounter Lu Boshe himself, returning cheerfully on his donkey with wine. Cao Cao, knowing the old man will discover the massacre and raise the alarm, draws his sword and kills him too. Chen Gong is appalled. Cao Cao delivers the line that will define his character for the rest of the series: "I would rather betray the entire world than let the world betray me" (宁教我负天下人, 休教天下人负我).

Rain begins to fall. In a chilling coda, Cao Cao suggests they return to the estate to eat the meal the servants had prepared, reasoning that the dead should not have died for nothing. He performs a solemn offering to Lu Boshe's spirit, blaming not himself but the chaos of the age. The next morning, Chen Gong quietly slips away, unable to remain with a man capable of such ruthlessness.

**Historical significance:** This episode dramatizes one of the most debated incidents in Three Kingdoms lore. Historical sources (the *Wei Shu*, *Shiyu*, and *Sun Sheng's Miscellaneous Records*) give contradictory accounts of what happened at Lu Boshe's home. The novel synthesized these into the version shown here. The famous quote about betraying the world appears in the novel but is not found in any historical record. The scene serves as a moral turning point: it reveals the cold pragmatism that will make Cao Cao both great and terrifying. Chen Gong's departure sets up their eventual reunion as enemies, one of the series' most powerful later arcs.
