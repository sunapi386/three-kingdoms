## Episode 85: Zhuge Liang Prepares for the Northern Campaigns

With the Memorial on Dispatching the Troops submitted to Emperor Liu Shan, Zhuge Liang begins assembling the Northern Expedition force. Before departing, he strips Li Yan of his personal troops and incorporates them into the expeditionary army, consolidating all military power under his direct control. He also receives intelligence that Meng Da, a former Shu general who had defected to Wei, is prepared to switch sides again.

On the Wei side, the young Emperor Cao Rui, despite the protests of the elder statesman Wang Lang, appoints the son of the late Xiahou Dun as Grand General with 200,000 troops to oppose the Shu invasion. Zhuge Liang's first strategic move is a masterful information operation: he spreads rumors designed to sow distrust between Sima Yi and the Wei court. Cao Rui takes the bait and personally travels to Yongliang to confront Sima Yi. Sima Yi, accompanied only by his sons, surrenders himself to Cao Rui and pleads for mercy. Cao Rui, moved by the old minister's apparent loyalty, spares his life but strips him of all military authority and sends him into retirement.

With Sima Yi neutralized, the Shu army surges forward, winning battle after battle against leaderless Wei forces. The climax of the episode is the confrontation between Zhuge Liang and Wang Lang before the assembled armies. Wang Lang rides out to persuade Zhuge Liang to surrender, delivering a lengthy speech about the mandate of heaven having passed to Wei. Zhuge Liang responds with a devastating verbal attack that systematically demolishes Wang Lang's arguments, his character, and the legitimacy of the Wei dynasty. The old minister, overwhelmed by rage and humiliation, falls from his horse and dies on the spot. Wei morale collapses. Cao Zhen attempts to exploit Wang Lang's death as a trap, but Zhuge Liang sees through the ruse and turns it into another Shu victory.

The episode also reveals that Zhuge Liang has successfully engineered Meng Da's defection back to Shu, setting up a potential second front deep inside Wei territory.

**Historical significance:** This episode compresses and dramatizes the opening moves of Zhuge Liang's first Northern Expedition (228 CE). The "scolding Wang Lang to death" (骂死王朗) is entirely fictional: the historical Wang Lang died of natural causes in 228, never confronted Zhuge Liang on any battlefield, and served as a distinguished minister. This scene is one of the novel's most famous inventions, showcasing Zhuge Liang's eloquence as a weapon. The Meng Da subplot is based on real events; Meng Da did secretly communicate with Zhuge Liang about defecting, but Sima Yi discovered the plot and acted with stunning speed to crush it. Notably, the 2010 series entirely omits the southern campaign against Meng Huo (七擒孟获), which occupied four full chapters in the novel and represents one of the most beloved storylines in the original text. The adaptation jumps directly from the consolidation of Shu to the Northern Expeditions.
