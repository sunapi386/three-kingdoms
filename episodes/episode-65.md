## Episode 65: The Plot Unravels

Tensions between Liu Bei and Liu Zhang continue to build. Liu Bei remains reluctant to move against his host, but Pang Tong devises a two-pronged strategy: he arranges for a forged letter to be delivered that frames Zhang Song as plotting to betray Liu Zhang to Liu Bei. The deception works perfectly. Liu Zhang, enraged at the apparent treachery, has Zhang Song arrested and executed. With Zhang Song dead, Liu Zhang turns hostile and mobilizes all of Yi Province's forces against Liu Bei.

Liu Bei, who genuinely valued Zhang Song, is devastated. Yet Pang Tong points out that the situation is now clarified: Liu Zhang has broken the peace and attacked first, giving Liu Bei moral justification to fight back. The two camps begin maneuvering for open war across the hills and valleys of western Sichuan. Pang Tong presses Liu Bei to strike quickly and march east toward the strategic city of Luocheng before Liu Zhang's forces can consolidate. Liu Bei, pushed by circumstance beyond his scruples, agrees. The stage is set for the decisive battle for Yi Province.

**Historical significance:** Zhang Song's execution is recorded in the historical sources. In both the novel and the histories, Zhang Song's conspiracy was discovered when his brother Zhang Su reported it to Liu Zhang. The show's version, which makes Pang Tong the architect of the exposure, heightens the moral complexity: Liu Bei's own side engineered the provocation that forced the war. This reflects a broader pattern in Three Kingdoms storytelling where Liu Bei's famous benevolence is shown to depend on strategists willing to do what he will not.
