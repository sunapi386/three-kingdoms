## Episode 9: The Phoenix Pavilion (凤仪亭)

The tension between Dong Zhuo and Lu Bu escalates toward a breaking point. Lu Bu, unable to stay away, sneaks into Dong Zhuo's estate while the tyrant is attending a court session and finds Diao Chan in the gardens at the Phoenix Pavilion (凤仪亭). In a tearful reunion, Diao Chan tells Lu Bu that Dong Zhuo has violated her, and Lu Bu is overcome with grief and rage.

Dong Zhuo returns unexpectedly and discovers them embracing. Roaring with fury, the massive warlord hurls his halberd at Lu Bu, who barely dodges in time. A chase ensues through the gardens, but the obese Dong Zhuo cannot keep up with the agile Lu Bu, who escapes with his life. Lu Bu flees to Wang Yun's residence in a panic, confessing that Dong Zhuo means to kill him.

Wang Yun, concealing his satisfaction, counsels patience. He advises Lu Bu to formally seek Diao Chan's hand through proper channels rather than hide. After Lu Bu leaves, Wang Yun discovers that Diao Chan's tears were not entirely an act: she has genuinely fallen in love with Lu Bu. This complicates the scheme but does not derail it.

Wang Yun then orchestrates another feast for Dong Zhuo, at which Diao Chan once again performs, this time in even more dazzling attire. Dong Zhuo is so delighted that he promotes Wang Yun and showers him with honors. But the rift between Dong Zhuo and Lu Bu is now irreparable. Wang Yun's stratagem has succeeded in transforming the most powerful warlord-bodyguard relationship in the realm into a jealous rivalry over one woman.

**Historical significance:** The Phoenix Pavilion scene is among the most dramatized moments in the entire Three Kingdoms tradition, featured in opera, film, and art for centuries. Historically, we know only that Lu Bu feared Dong Zhuo's wrath after conducting an affair with one of his women, and that this fear was one factor in his later betrayal. The elaborate scheming by Wang Yun is the novel's invention. In real history, Wang Yun was indeed the chief conspirator behind Dong Zhuo's assassination, but he worked through political channels rather than romantic manipulation. The 2010 series deepens the emotional stakes by making Diao Chan's love for Lu Bu genuine, adding moral complexity to a character traditionally portrayed as a calculating seductress.
