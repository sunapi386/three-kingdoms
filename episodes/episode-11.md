## Episode 11: Tao Qian Offers Xuzhou Thrice (陶恭祖三让徐州城)

Word reaches Cao Cao that his father Cao Song has been murdered while traveling through Xuzhou province. Cao Song had been en route to join his son in Yan Province when Zhang Kai, a subordinate of Xuzhou's governor Tao Qian, killed him and his entire retinue for their wealth. Cao Cao is shattered by grief, but his chief advisor Xun Yu sees the opportunity clearly: heaven itself has handed Cao Cao the pretext to seize Xuzhou.

Cao Cao mobilizes his full army and marches on Xuzhou under the banner of filial revenge. The elderly Tao Qian, dressed in mourning whites and accompanied by his sons, meets Cao Cao outside the city walls to beg forgiveness, taking personal responsibility for the crime. Cao Cao is unmoved. He declares he will take the city within three days. Tao Qian realizes that Cao Cao's grief is real but the invasion is strategic: revenge is the excuse, but conquest is the goal.

Desperate, Tao Qian sends urgent appeals for help to Yuan Shao, Yuan Shu, and other regional lords. Every one of them responds with empty sympathy and polite excuses, content to sit back and watch Xuzhou fall. The episode establishes a pattern that will recur throughout the series: in the post-coalition world, no one lifts a finger unless the outcome directly benefits them.

**Historical significance:** Cao Song's murder and Cao Cao's punitive campaigns against Xuzhou (193 and 194 AD) are well documented in historical sources. The Records of the Three Kingdoms and other texts note that Cao Cao's forces carried out brutal massacres of civilians during the Xuzhou campaigns, a detail this adaptation significantly softens. Tao Qian's repeated offers to cede the province to Liu Bei are drawn from Chapter 11 of the novel, with the "thrice declining" formula following the classical Chinese literary convention of refusing a gift three times before accepting. Historically, Tao Qian did favor Liu Bei as a successor, but the ritualized three-fold offer is a novelistic embellishment.
