## Episode 75: The Abdication

Sima Yi analyzes the political conditions and concludes the time is ripe for Cao Pi to formally replace the Han dynasty. Cao Pi agrees, but Sima Yi insists the initiative must appear to come from others. He arranges for the minister Hua Xin to lead a group of officials in demanding Emperor Xian's abdication in open court.

The Emperor, surrounded by hostile ministers and soldiers, visits the imperial temple in despair. Cao Hong and Cao Xiu arrive with armed troops to accelerate the process. Under this combined military and political pressure, Emperor Xian surrenders the imperial seal. The humiliation is total. Outside the palace, Cao Pi encounters his mother, Empress Dowager Bian, who bitterly rebukes him for destroying the dynasty their family served. Cao Pi shows no remorse.

In a scene that underlines the cruelty of the transition, Cao Pi rejects the first draft of the abdication edict, finding its language insufficiently submissive. He forces Emperor Xian to rewrite it, again and again, each draft more degrading than the last. That night, Sima Yi visits the broken emperor and reveals that Hua Xin has already begun constructing the abdication platform, the "Altar of the Yielded Mandate" (受禅台). The formality is a performance; the outcome was decided long ago.

**Historical significance:** The abdication of Emperor Xian on November 25, 220 CE, ended the Han dynasty after over four centuries. The historical process of "abdication by yielding" (禅让) was ostensibly voluntary and modeled on the ancient sage-kings Yao and Shun, but everyone understood it was coerced. This "polite usurpation" would become the standard template for dynastic transitions in Chinese history for centuries afterward. Hua Xin's role as the frontman pressuring the emperor is historically documented. The TV series dramatizes the repeated rewriting of the edict and adds the drowning of Emperor Xian (in the next episode), which is fictional; the historical Emperor Xian lived another 14 years as the Duke of Shanyang and died of natural causes in 234 CE.
