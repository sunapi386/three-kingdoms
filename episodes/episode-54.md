## Episode 54: The Ganlu Temple

In Sun Shangxiang's chambers, Lady Wu tells her daughter that if she approves of Liu Bei, she should play the jade flute left behind by her father Sun Jian as a signal. At the Ganlu Temple, Lu Meng has hidden armed men, waiting for Liu Bei's arrival. Despite Zhao Yun's protests, Liu Bei insists on entering the temple with only a small personal guard. Lady Wu is immediately taken with Liu Bei's dignified bearing and noble demeanor.

Zhao Yun publicly exposes Lu Meng's hidden ambush, throwing the proceedings into chaos. Sun Quan moves to execute Lu Meng for the embarrassment, but Liu Bei magnanimously intercedes to save him. Liu Bei then delivers an impassioned speech about his lifelong dedication to restoring the Han dynasty, moving everyone present. At the crucial moment, the sound of Sun Shangxiang's flute drifts down from her chambers, signaling her approval. The wedding proceeds. On the wedding night, Liu Bei finds Sun Shangxiang's chambers lined with weapons and armed maids. She challenges him to a martial contest before accepting him as her husband.

Zhou Yu visits Lady Wu and persuades her to keep Liu Bei and his new wife in Jianye for an extended stay. Liu Bei sees through this as Zhou Yu's ploy to keep him away from Jingzhou, but deliberately plays along, reasoning that the longer he stays in Wu, the more time Zhuge Liang has to consolidate Jingzhou's defenses. Zhou Yu decides to use a life of luxury and indulgence to weaken Liu Bei's will, and Liu Bei cooperates fully, neglecting even his riding and archery practice.

**Historical significance:** The Ganlu Temple scene is one of the most theatrical set pieces in the Romance of the Three Kingdoms and is entirely fictional. Sun Shangxiang's portrayal as a martial woman who keeps weapons in her chambers does have some basis in the historical records: the Sanguozhi notes that she had over a hundred armed female attendants and that Liu Bei felt uneasy around her. The flute signal, the ambush, and Liu Bei's speech are novelistic inventions designed to showcase Liu Bei's charisma and moral authority. The "honey trap" strategy of softening Liu Bei with luxury reflects a real concern: the novel's point is that even a hero can be dulled by comfort, a theme that recurs throughout the Three Kingdoms narrative.
