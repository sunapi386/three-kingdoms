## Episode 88: The Death of Zhao Yun

The episode opens with one of the most poignant farewells in the series. Zhao Yun, the last of the Five Tiger Generals, falls ill and dies. In the drama's telling, his final words are a repeated cry of "Northern Expedition! Northern Expedition! Northern Expedition!" before he passes. Zhuge Liang, receiving the news, breaks down completely. "All my Tiger Generals are gone," he grieves. Zhao Yun's death severs the last living link to the brotherhood of Liu Bei, Guan Yu, and Zhang Fei that founded Shu-Han. From this point forward, Zhuge Liang carries the mission alone.

Steeling himself through grief, Zhuge Liang launches the second Northern Expedition. The target is Chencang, a heavily fortified Wei stronghold held by the determined garrison commander Hao Zhao. The siege proves costly and frustrating as Hao Zhao's defenses hold firm against repeated Shu assaults. Zhuge Liang shifts strategy, using Jiang Wei (a talented young Wei defector who has become his most promising protege) to lure Cao Zhen into a trap through a feigned defection. Cao Zhen falls for the deception and leads his forces into an ambush. Wei suffers significant losses.

However, the fundamental problem of the Northern Expeditions reasserts itself: supply lines. Shu's armies must cross mountain passes on narrow roads, and they simply cannot sustain a long campaign. With grain running low and no prospect of a quick victory at Chencang, Zhuge Liang orders a withdrawal southward. Cao Zhen, learning of the Shu retreat, eagerly pursues. During the withdrawal, Wei Yan turns and counterattacks, killing the Wei general Wang Shuang and briefly recapturing Chencang. The garrison commander Hao Zhao, seeing the situation is hopeless, takes his own life. Despite these tactical successes, the strategic reality remains: the second Northern Expedition has failed to achieve its objectives.

**Historical significance:** Zhao Yun died in 229 CE. The deathbed cry of "Northern Expedition!" is a dramatic invention, but his death during this period is historical. His passing symbolized the end of an era. The siege of Chencang (228-229 CE) is based on real events: Hao Zhao's defense of Chencang against Zhuge Liang's forces is recorded in the histories and was considered a brilliant example of defensive warfare. However, the historical Hao Zhao died of illness after the siege, not by suicide. Wang Shuang's death is historical. Jiang Wei's defection to Shu is also historical (228 CE) and Zhuge Liang did indeed see tremendous potential in the young officer, eventually grooming him as his military successor. The chronic supply problem that plagued every Northern Expedition was the central strategic reality that Zhuge Liang could never overcome.
