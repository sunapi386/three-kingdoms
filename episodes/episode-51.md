## Episode 51: The Jingzhou Debt

In Sun Quan's camp, Lu Su and Sun Quan discuss how to reclaim Jingzhou from Liu Bei. Reports arrive that Liu Bei is actively expanding his military, and Sun Quan immediately dispatches Lu Su to first visit the ailing Zhou Yu at Baling, then proceed to Jingzhou to formally demand its return. At the Wu army camp, Zhou Yu's illness has worsened, but his hatred of Zhuge Liang has only intensified. Lu Su's visit hardens Zhou Yu's resolve to recapture Jingzhou by force if necessary.

In Jingzhou, Lu Su presents his case for the return of the province before the memorial tablet of Liu Qi (Liu Biao's son, whose death removed Liu Bei's last pretext for holding the territory). Zhuge Liang engages Lu Su in a sharp debate over Jingzhou's rightful ownership, and the two finally reach a written agreement: Liu Bei will return Jingzhou to Eastern Wu once he has conquered the western province of Yi (Sichuan). Zhou Yu dismisses this agreement as worthless paper and refuses to abandon his plans for a military solution.

Days later, news arrives that Lady Gan, Liu Bei's first wife, has died. Zhou Yu seizes on this as an opportunity: he proposes using Sun Quan's younger sister, Lady Sun (Sun Shangxiang), as bait to lure Liu Bei to the Wu capital of Jianye, where he can be taken hostage and exchanged for Jingzhou.

**Historical significance:** The dispute over Jingzhou is one of the pivotal conflicts of the Three Kingdoms period. Historically, Liu Bei did "borrow" Jingzhou's southern commanderies after Red Cliffs (208 CE), and the question of when or whether he would return them became a chronic source of tension between the Sun-Liu alliance. The written agreement to return Jingzhou after taking Yi Province appears in the novel Romance of the Three Kingdoms but is not well attested in historical records. The death of Lady Gan is historical, and the marriage plot involving Sun Shangxiang is one of the novel's most famous storylines. In real history, the marriage was a genuine political alliance arranged by Sun Quan (not primarily a trap), though later tradition and the novel reframed it as Zhou Yu's scheme.
