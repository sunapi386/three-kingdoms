## Episode 70: The Death of Yang Xiu

The Hanzhong campaign reaches its climax. Huang Zhong leads Liu Bei's forces in an assault on Mount Dingjun, the key stronghold defending Hanzhong. The garrison commander Xiahou Yuan, one of Cao Cao's most capable generals, fights a desperate defense and sends urgent pleas for reinforcement. Cao Cao dispatches a relief column, but it arrives too late. Huang Zhong, the aging warrior, leads a charge that kills Xiahou Yuan on the mountain, securing Mount Dingjun for Liu Bei and breaking Cao Cao's hold on Hanzhong.

Liu Bei presses his advantage, committing his entire army to the Hanzhong theater. Cao Cao's forces are drawn into a grinding war of attrition. Frustrated and suffering from his worsening headaches, Cao Cao issues the cryptic nighttime password "chicken ribs" (ji lei). Yang Xiu, the brilliant but reckless aide, instantly deciphers the meaning: chicken ribs are tasteless to eat but a waste to throw away, just as Hanzhong has become for Cao Cao. Yang Xiu tells his comrades to pack their belongings for a retreat to Xuchang. When Cao Cao discovers that Yang Xiu has broadcast his private indecision to the entire camp, he executes him for sowing disorder. The real offense, however, is deeper: Yang Xiu had long supported Cao Zhi in the succession struggle, and his death is also a political purge.

The campaign turns decisively against Cao Cao. His headaches worsen, and Sima Yi, ever the pragmatist, orders the withdrawal to Xuchang. Liu Bei's followers urge him to proclaim himself King of Hanzhong, and he does so, reaching the pinnacle of his power. But Zhuge Liang watches the celebrations with deep unease, sensing that overconfidence will lead to disaster. In Jing Province, Guan Yu is emboldened by Liu Bei's victories and resolves to attack the Cao Wei fortress at Fan Castle, hoping to coordinate a grand north-south offensive. His decision to march north will have catastrophic consequences.

**Historical significance:** The Battle of Mount Dingjun (219 CE) and Xiahou Yuan's death are among the most important military events of the Three Kingdoms period. Huang Zhong's slaying of Xiahou Yuan is historical, and it was the decisive moment that gave Liu Bei control of Hanzhong. Yang Xiu's execution over the "chicken ribs" incident is one of the most famous anecdotes in Chinese literature, illustrating both Cao Cao's ruthlessness and the danger of being too clever at court. Historically, Yang Xiu's death was tied to his involvement in the Cao Zhi faction. Liu Bei's self-proclamation as King of Hanzhong in 219 CE marked the high-water mark of his career. Guan Yu's subsequent attack on Fan Castle, while initially successful, would lead to the loss of Jing Province and his own death, fundamentally altering the trajectory of the Three Kingdoms era.
