## Episode 39: Jiang Gan Steals a Letter

Zhuge Liang's brother Zhuge Jin, sent by Zhou Yu, visits the Southland camp to persuade Zhuge Liang to defect to Wu's service. Before he can even make the pitch, Zhuge Liang turns the conversation around and urges his brother to join Liu Bei instead. Zhuge Jin returns empty-handed, and Zhou Yu's determination to eliminate Zhuge Liang only grows.

Meanwhile, Liu Bei grows anxious about Zhuge Liang's prolonged absence in the east and sends messengers. Zhuge Liang replies with a cryptic instruction: have Zhao Yun bring a small boat to the southern shore each day after the twentieth of the eleventh month.

On Cao Cao's side, the question of how to break the Sun-Liu alliance leads to a volunteer: Jiang Gan, a scholar who claims to be an old classmate of Zhou Yu's. He crosses the river to Zhou Yu's camp, confident he can talk his former friend into switching sides. Zhou Yu sees through the ploy instantly and turns it into an opportunity. He throws an extravagant banquet, feigns drunkenness, and "accidentally" leaves forged documents in his tent that implicate Cao Cao's naval commanders Cai Mao and Zhang Yun as secret agents working for Wu. Jiang Gan, believing he has stumbled onto priceless intelligence, steals the letters and hurries back to Cao Cao's camp.

**Historical significance:** Jiang Gan was a real person who did visit Zhou Yu, but the historical account says only that Zhou Yu refused to be swayed and that Jiang Gan returned praising Zhou Yu's character. The entire "stealing a letter" ruse is a novel invention, one of the most famous counter-intelligence plots in Chinese literature. The real Cai Mao and Zhang Yun did serve Cao Cao after the surrender of Jingzhou, but whether they were executed and the circumstances differ from the novel's version. This episode showcases the novel's fascination with deception and psychological warfare, themes that dominate the entire Red Cliffs sequence.
