## Episode 13: Cao Cao Rescues the Emperor (曹孟德救驾令诸侯)

Two major developments unfold in parallel. In the west, Emperor Xian has escaped from the warlords Li Jue and Guo Si, who had seized power in Chang'an after Dong Zhuo's assassination. The young emperor and a small retinue of loyalists, including the minister Dong Cheng, are fleeing eastward toward the old capital of Luoyang, sending desperate edicts to every warlord in the realm begging them to come to his rescue.

Cao Cao, having recaptured much of Yan Province from Lu Bu, immediately recognizes this as the opportunity of a lifetime. He mobilizes his army and races toward Luoyang. Meanwhile, Yuan Shao also receives the emperor's edict. His advisor Xu You urges him to seize the emperor, arguing that whoever holds the Son of Heaven holds the mandate of the realm. But other advisors counter that rescuing a powerless puppet is more trouble than it is worth, and Yuan Shao, fatally indecisive, does nothing.

Cao Cao arrives at the ruined Luoyang to find the emperor and court starving and destitute. He presents them with food, supplies, and the protection of his army. The grateful emperor weeps. The next day, Cao Cao accompanies the emperor to review his impressive troops, then delicately proposes relocating the capital to Xuchang, deep within Cao Cao's own territory. The ministers exchange uneasy glances: they have escaped one tyrant only to fall into the hands of another. Once in Xuchang, Cao Cao wastes no time. Using the emperor's seal, he issues a cascade of imperial edicts that formally assign territories to every warlord in the realm, effectively controlling the political chessboard. Yuan Shao, who passed on his chance, is reduced to accepting his title through gritted teeth.

**Historical significance:** The relocation of the emperor to Xuchang in 196 AD is one of the most consequential events in the entire Three Kingdoms period. Cao Cao's strategy of "using the Son of Heaven to command the lords" (挟天子以令诸侯) gave him a decisive advantage that no rival could match: the ability to issue orders with the stamp of imperial legitimacy. This was Xun Yu's counsel in the historical record, and it transformed Cao Cao from a regional strongman into the de facto ruler of China. Yuan Shao's failure to act on Xu You's advice is regarded by historians as one of his greatest blunders. The episode dramatizes this turning point faithfully, though it compresses events that unfolded over several months into a single dramatic sequence.
