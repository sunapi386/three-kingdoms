## Episode 86: The Loss of Jieting and the Empty Fort

Sima Yi, despite his political disgrace, receives urgent intelligence: Meng Da has betrayed Wei and plans to seize the city of Xincheng as a staging ground for Shu. An old acquaintance named Shen Yi tips him off. Sima Yi acts without waiting for imperial authorization, riding through the night with an elite cavalry force. He reaches Xincheng before Meng Da's preparations are complete, storms the city, and executes the traitor. Sima Yi presents Meng Da's head to Emperor Cao Rui, who is so impressed by this display of initiative and loyalty that he restores Sima Yi to full military command, granting him authority over the armies defending both capitals.

Sima Yi immediately identifies the critical vulnerability in Zhuge Liang's campaign: the supply road through Jieting (Street Pavilion). Whoever holds Jieting controls whether the Shu army eats or starves. Ma Su, Zhuge Liang's protege, volunteers to defend this crucial chokepoint and signs a military oath guaranteeing success. When Ma Su arrives at Jieting, however, he ignores his deputy Wang Ping's protests and stations the entire force on top of a hill rather than guarding the road below. Sima Yi is overjoyed when he learns of this blunder. He surrounds the hill, cuts off the water supply, and shatters the Shu garrison. Wang Ping's small force fights bravely but cannot prevent the disaster.

Zhuge Liang, receiving Ma Su's deployment map, immediately recognizes that Jieting is lost and issues a cascade of emergency orders. But the damage is done: without Jieting, the entire Northern Expedition is unsustainable. Zhuge Liang personally travels to the small city of Xicheng to oversee the evacuation of supplies. When Sima Yi's main force unexpectedly appears outside the virtually undefended city, Zhuge Liang makes a desperate gamble. He orders the city gates thrown open, stations a few soldiers disguised as street sweepers outside, ascends the city wall, and begins playing the qin (zither). Sima Yi, suspecting an elaborate ambush from the man he considers the most dangerous strategist alive, orders a full retreat. The Empty Fort Strategy buys Zhuge Liang just enough time to complete the evacuation.

**Historical significance:** The loss of Jieting (228 CE) was a genuine and pivotal disaster for Shu. Ma Su's decision to camp on the hilltop against orders is confirmed in historical sources, and his execution afterward is also historical. However, the Empty Fort Strategy (空城计) is almost certainly fictional. It does not appear in the official histories (Chen Shou's Records of the Three Kingdoms) and was added by the novel. The historical Sima Yi was not even at Jieting; he was dealing with Meng Da at the time. The show combines two separate events (Sima Yi crushing Meng Da and the Jieting campaign) into a single, streamlined narrative. Despite being fictional, the Empty Fort Strategy has become one of the most famous military anecdotes in Chinese culture and one of the Thirty-Six Stratagems.
