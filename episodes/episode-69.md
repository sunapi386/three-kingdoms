## Episode 69: The Empty Box

Cao Cao, now King of Wei in all but name, pushes further to consolidate his authority. When he attempts to tear down the White Horse Gate as a symbol of the old imperial order, Xun Yu is outraged. The man who spent decades as Cao Cao's most trusted strategist can no longer reconcile his loyalty to his lord with his loyalty to the Han dynasty. Xun Yu falls ill, consumed by the impossible contradiction of his position. Cao Cao sends his advisor Cheng Yu to visit the sick man and delivers what appears to be a gift: a box of fine fruits. Xun Yu opens the box and finds it empty. He understands the message immediately: there is nothing left for you here. Xun Yu laughs, then takes his own life.

At Xun Yu's funeral, Cao Cao and Cao Pi attend in person, displaying extravagant grief. But the political machinery does not pause. Pro-Han loyalists in the capital, led by the official Geng Ji, plot a coup against Cao Cao's regime. The conspiracy is discovered, and it falls to Cao Pi to suppress the rebellion. He does so with decisive brutality, proving his capability as a political and military leader. Cao Cao, observing his son's performance, begins to tilt the succession in Cao Pi's favor over the more literary Cao Zhi. The episode closes with Cao Cao's attention turning south toward the strategic region of Hanzhong, where Liu Bei's growing power threatens to upset the balance between the three kingdoms.

**Historical significance:** Xun Yu's death in 212 CE is one of the most poignant episodes in Three Kingdoms history. The empty box is a famous detail from the novel (in some versions, the box contained food but was empty, symbolizing "there is no more Han to serve"). Historically, Xun Yu died of illness shortly after opposing Cao Cao's elevation to Duke of Wei, and many historians believe he was driven to suicide or poisoned. Xun Yu's tragedy embodies the impossible position of those who served Cao Cao believing they were restoring order to the Han empire, only to realize they were building its replacement. Geng Ji's rebellion (218 CE) is a real historical event; placing it here allows the show to showcase Cao Pi's political competence. The succession struggle between Cao Pi and Cao Zhi was one of the defining dramas of Wei's early years.
