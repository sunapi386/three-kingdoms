## Episode 94: The Chancellor's Death at Wuzhang Plains

Atop the hill overlooking the failed fire attack, Zhuge Liang collapses, coughing blood. In his tent at the Shu camp, gravely ill and knowing his time is short, he entrusts the future of the state to Jiang Wei and Yang Yi. Wei Yan forces his way into the tent against the guards' objections. Zhuge Liang, in a calculated final act, pretends to hand over supreme military command to Wei Yan, who accepts. As soon as Wei Yan leaves, Zhuge Liang secretly summons Ma Dai and orders him to eliminate Wei Yan when the time comes.

In the twelfth year of Jianxing (234 AD), Zhuge Liang dies at Wuzhang Plains, his lifelong dream of restoring the Han dynasty unfulfilled. Before his funeral altar, Wei Yan seizes the commander's seal and orders the army to continue the Northern Expedition. Ma Dai strikes, killing Wei Yan in front of the altar, and command passes to Yang Yi. The Shu army begins its withdrawal south, following Zhuge Liang's final instructions.

Sima Yi orders his forces to pursue in three columns. But at the Chencang road crossing, the Wei army rides into an ambush. Jiang Wei wheels out a wooden effigy of Zhuge Liang mounted in his signature four-wheeled chariot. Sima Yi, terrified that Zhuge Liang might have faked his own death as a trap, panics and flees with his entire army. The Shu forces withdraw safely to Hanzhong. This incident gives rise to the famous saying: "A dead Zhuge scares away a living Sima" (死诸葛吓走活仲达).

Back in Wei, Emperor Cao Rui orders Sima Yi to surrender his military authority to Xiahou Ba and report to the capital. Sima Yi departs without protest, but his soldiers chase after him, swearing their eternal loyalty. The seeds of the Sima family's future power are already planted.

**Historical significance:** Zhuge Liang's death at Wuzhang Plains in the autumn of 234 AD (the eighth month of Jianxing 12 by the lunar calendar) is one of the most significant events in Three Kingdoms history. He was 53 years old. His final words and arrangements are among the most retold scenes in Chinese literature. The killing of Wei Yan is partly historical and partly dramatized. In the novel, Zhuge Liang prophesied Wei Yan's rebellion and arranged his death in advance. Historically, the situation was more ambiguous: after Zhuge Liang died, Wei Yan and Yang Yi each accused the other of treason, and Wei Yan was killed by Ma Dai during the resulting power struggle. Many historians believe Wei Yan was treated unjustly and that Yang Yi manipulated events. The wooden effigy ruse and Sima Yi's panicked retreat appear in the novel but have a basis in the historical accounts, which record that the Shu army's orderly withdrawal did indeed startle Sima Yi into retreating. The "dead Zhuge scares a living Sima" proverb became one of the most enduring expressions in Chinese culture.
