## Episode 6: The Imperial Jade Seal (孙坚得玉玺)

Cao Cao borrows four thousand cavalry from Sun Jian under a pretext and rides ahead to pursue Dong Zhuo, who is forcibly marching the Emperor, the court, and the people of Luoyang westward to Chang'an. Dong Zhuo's generals Li Jue and Guo Si provide a rear guard, and an ambush in the mountain passes inflicts heavy losses on Cao Cao's pursuing force. During the chaos, Lu Bu abandons his duty protecting the imperial carriage to instead rescue Diao Chan, revealing where his true loyalties lie.

Sun Jian, accompanied by his sons Sun Ce and Sun Quan and his veteran officers Cheng Pu and Huang Gai, enters the ruined capital of Luoyang. In the wreckage of the great palace, they discover the legendary Imperial Jade Seal (传国玉玺), the symbol of heavenly mandate to rule, hidden within the imperial throne. Cheng Pu and Sun Quan explain its centuries-old history. Sun Jian is overcome with excitement at possessing it.

When the coalition lords reconvene in the ruins of Luoyang to celebrate their "victory," the atmosphere quickly sours. Cao Cao, Liu Bei, and Sun Jian each express disgust with the alliance's incompetence and self-interest. Yuan Shu accuses Sun Jian of secretly keeping the Jade Seal. A tense standoff follows, with both sides producing hidden soldiers. Sun Jian seizes Yuan Shao as a hostage, forcing him to publicly deny the accusation. The anti-Dong Zhuo coalition officially collapses, and each lord departs to pursue his own ambitions.

**Historical significance:** Sun Jian's discovery of the Jade Seal is from the novel; historical sources differ on who found it and when. The Seal's symbolic importance was real: possessing it implied a claim to imperial legitimacy, and it became a recurring plot device in both the novel and historical accounts. The coalition's disintegration is well documented historically. Yuan Shao and Yuan Shu's rivalry was genuine and rooted in their complex family dynamics (one was the legitimate heir, the other the son of a concubine). Cao Cao's pursuit of Dong Zhuo and subsequent defeat at Xingyang is historical, and it deepened his contempt for the coalition lords who refused to commit their forces.
