## Episode 68: Guan Yu Attends a Feast Armed with Only a Blade

With Liu Bei now master of Yi Province, the question of Jing Province becomes urgent. Sun Quan and Lu Su discuss how to reclaim the three commanderies that Liu Bei had borrowed. Sun Quan sends Zhuge Liang's brother, Zhuge Jin, to Chengdu to request the return of the territory, but Guan Yu, who holds Jing Province, flatly refuses to hand over even a single commandery and drives away Wu's appointed officials by force.

Lu Su, gravely ill, makes one final personal effort. He invites Guan Yu to a banquet to negotiate. Guan Yu arrives alone, armed with nothing but his Green Dragon Crescent Blade, in the famous episode known as "attending the feast with a single blade" (dan dao fu hui). At the banquet, Lu Su and Guan Yu engage in a fierce verbal duel over the rightful ownership of Jing Province. At the critical moment, Guan Yu, moved by Lu Su's sincerity and integrity, agrees to return the three southern commanderies of Changsha, Guiyang, and Lingling to Wu. Lu Su, his mission accomplished but his health broken, passes away soon after. On his deathbed, he implores Lu Meng to maintain the alliance with Liu Bei against Cao Cao as the foundational strategy of Wu.

In Xuchang, a different crisis is unfolding. Court officials petition Emperor Xian to grant Cao Cao the title of King of Wei. Only the venerable Xun Yu stands in opposition, arguing passionately against the elevation. The emperor, powerless, grants the title anyway. Outside the city gates, Cao Zhi rides recklessly through the restricted White Horse Gate, violating imperial protocol. Xun Yu confronts him with a furious rebuke, but the incident only deepens the growing rift between the old loyalist guard and Cao Cao's increasingly imperial household.

**Historical significance:** The "Single Blade Feast" (dan dao fu hui, ~215 CE) is one of Guan Yu's most famous episodes. Historically, it was actually Lu Su who invited Guan Yu and aggressively pressed the case for returning Jing Province, with Lu Su being the assertive party rather than the gentle diplomat of the novel. Lu Su's death in 217 CE was a turning point: his policy of alliance with Liu Bei died with him, and Lu Meng would pursue a far more aggressive approach. Cao Cao's elevation to King of Wei in 216 CE was a watershed moment signaling that the Han dynasty existed in name only. Xun Yu's opposition is placed slightly earlier in the show for dramatic effect but reflects his genuine historical stance against Cao Cao's imperial pretensions.
