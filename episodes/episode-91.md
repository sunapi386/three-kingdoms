## Episode 91: The Forged Letter

The armies of Shu and Wei clash on the battlefield, and Zhuge Liang deploys a formation so brilliant that Sima Yi is left speechless. The Wei troops charge in recklessly and are routed. Jiang Wei urges an immediate pursuit, but Zhuge Liang hesitates: the supply wagons have not arrived. The supply officer Gou An has been negligent, delaying provisions by several days. By rights the offense warrants execution, but Zhuge Liang stays his hand because Gou An is the nephew of Li Yan, one of the two regents entrusted by the late Emperor Liu Bei. He settles for a lesser punishment and sends Gou An back toward Chengdu.

On the road home, Gou An is captured by Wei soldiers and brought before Sima Yi, who sees an opportunity. He orders Gou An to carry a forged letter back to Shu designed to drive a wedge between Li Yan and Zhuge Liang. The scheme works. Li Yan presents the letter to Emperor Liu Shan and accuses Zhuge Liang of harboring treasonous ambitions. The alarmed young emperor orders Zhuge Liang to abandon his campaign and return to the capital immediately. Zhuge Liang complies, traveling back to Chengdu alone. In a private audience, Li Yan confronts him with the letter. Zhuge Liang calmly dismantles the forgery, exposing Gou An's betrayal. Liu Shan has Li Yan stripped of rank and imprisoned, reduced to a commoner. In a gesture that astonishes the court, Zhuge Liang then recommends Li Yan's son, Li Feng, for the post of Director of the Imperial Secretariat. With the crisis resolved, Zhuge Liang prepares to return to the front lines at Qishan.

**Historical significance:** This episode dramatizes events from Zhuge Liang's Fourth Northern Expedition (231 AD). The historical Li Yan (also known as Li Ping) was indeed one of two regents appointed by Liu Bei on his deathbed, alongside Zhuge Liang. In the historical record, Li Yan failed to deliver supplies due to heavy rains and then tried to cover his failure by shifting blame onto Zhuge Liang, claiming the retreat was unauthorized. Zhuge Liang produced Li Yan's own earlier correspondence proving the deception, and Li Yan was exiled. The forged-letter plot involving Gou An and Sima Yi is a dramatic invention from the novel "Romance of the Three Kingdoms," but the underlying power struggle between Zhuge Liang and Li Yan, and the supply crisis that forced a withdrawal, are historical. Zhuge Liang's magnanimity toward Li Yan's son is attested in the records and speaks to his reputation for principled governance. Li Yan reportedly died of grief in 234 upon hearing of Zhuge Liang's death, having held out hope that Zhuge Liang would eventually recall him from exile.
