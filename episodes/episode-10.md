## Episode 10: The Death of Dong Zhuo (董卓之死)

Wang Yun returns home to find Chen Gong, his old friend and Cao Cao's former companion, waiting for him. Chen Gong has seen through the chain stratagem and names it openly, but rather than expose it, he offers his support. He argues that Lu Bu, the greatest warrior alive, can be turned into a weapon for righteous men if properly handled. As they confer, Lu Bu himself bursts in, frantic and afraid. Wang Yun and Chen Gong together persuade him that killing Dong Zhuo is not betrayal but duty: it is the act of a hero liberating the Emperor and the people. Lu Bu swears an oath to heaven that he will destroy the tyrant.

Wang Yun devises the final trap. He and the court officials pretend to endorse Dong Zhuo's ambition to declare himself Emperor, organizing an elaborate enthronement ceremony. On the appointed day, as Dong Zhuo arrives at the palace in full imperial regalia, Wang Yun gives the signal. Lu Bu steps forward and drives his halberd through Dong Zhuo's body. The tyrant dies in the street, and the common people of Chang'an celebrate in the streets, literally lighting candles in Dong Zhuo's navel fat, so hated was he.

The victory is short-lived. Wang Yun, intoxicated by success, ignores Chen Gong's counsel to offer amnesty to Dong Zhuo's remaining generals, Li Jue and Guo Si. Instead, he rebuffs their surrender. Desperate and with nothing to lose, Li Jue and Guo Si rally their Western Liang troops and attack the poorly defended capital. They capture the Emperor, and Wang Yun, refusing to flee, throws himself from the city wall in a final act of defiance.

In a parallel storyline, the episode introduces the first stirrings of Cao Cao's rise to power. Tao Qian, the governor of Xu Province, attempts to curry favor with Cao Cao by hosting his father Cao Song during a journey. But the escort troops, tempted by the Cao family's wealth, murder Cao Song on the road. This sets the stage for Cao Cao's vengeful invasion of Xu Province. Lu Bu and Diao Chan consummate their marriage at Meiwu, sleeping in the bed that once belonged to Dong Zhuo.

**Historical significance:** Dong Zhuo's assassination by Lu Bu in 192 AD is one of the most consequential events of the late Han period. The historical account largely matches the dramatic version: Wang Yun orchestrated a plot, and Lu Bu killed Dong Zhuo during what was supposed to be a ceremony. The people of Chang'an did celebrate, and historical sources confirm the gruesome detail about lighting Dong Zhuo's body fat as a candle. However, the aftermath played out as shown: Wang Yun's refusal to pardon Li Jue and Guo Si was a catastrophic political blunder that led to the sack of Chang'an and years of further chaos. Wang Yun's death (historical, 192 AD) marked the end of any hope for restoring central authority through the old court aristocracy. The murder of Cao Song (historically placed in 193 or 194 AD) was the trigger for Cao Cao's brutal campaign against Xu Province, one of the bloodiest episodes of the era. The series compresses these events to maintain narrative momentum.
