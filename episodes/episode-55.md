## Episode 55: The Escape from Wu

As the year draws to a close, Liu Bei has grown visibly soft and complacent in his Eastern Wu residence, indulging in luxury and demanding ever-larger monthly stipends from Sun Quan. Zhou Yu is delighted by this decline. Zhao Yun, unable to bear watching his lord's deterioration, publicly confronts a drunken Liu Bei, and the two quarrel bitterly. Liu Bei angrily orders Zhao Yun to return to Jingzhou.

Back in Jingzhou, Zhuge Liang punishes Zhao Yun with thirty strokes of the military rod, then immediately orders him to return to Jiangdong to protect Liu Bei. Guan Yu and Zhang Fei advocate a surprise attack on Jianye to rescue their brother, but Zhuge Liang refuses. Zhou Yu dispatches Lu Meng to spread rumors in Jingzhou that Zhuge Liang has made a secret pact with Zhou Yu and intends to seize power for himself.

In his Wu residence, Liu Bei and Zhao Yun secretly discuss plans to return to Jingzhou. Liu Bei decides to use a fabricated report of Cao Cao threatening Jingzhou as his pretext for leaving. Sun Shangxiang senses the deception but helps by requesting Lady Wu's permission to leave the city under the cover of visiting Liu Bei's ancestral graves. In Jingzhou, Zhang Fei drunkenly rampages, berating Zhuge Liang for failing to rescue Liu Bei, and overturns Zhuge Liang's command desk. Zhuge Liang endures the humiliation for the sake of the greater cause. At a banquet, Lady Wu deliberately gets Sun Quan drunk, allowing Liu Bei's party to slip out of the city. But Zhou Yu has already set up an ambush along the escape route.

**Historical significance:** The "escape from Wu" sequence draws loosely on real events. Historically, Liu Bei did eventually leave Wu territory with Sun Shangxiang, but the circumstances were less dramatic than the novel depicts. The quarrel between Zhao Yun and Liu Bei, the thirty strokes punishment, and the elaborate escape plan are fictional additions. Zhang Fei's insubordination toward Zhuge Liang reflects a recurring novelistic theme about the tension between Liu Bei's sworn brotherhood and his reliance on his chief strategist. The rumor campaign is the novel's way of showing Zhou Yu as a master of psychological warfare, though historically there is little evidence of such specific operations.
