## Episode 29: Fire at Wuchao

Cao Cao rushes out barefoot to welcome Xu You, overjoyed at his old friend's arrival. Xu You asks how long Cao Cao's grain can last. Cao Cao lies repeatedly, claiming one year, then half a year, three months, half a month, ten days. Xu You laughs and calls him the "ancestor of all villains." Cao Cao finally admits the truth: he has only three days of supplies left. Xu You lays out the plan: a night raid on Wuchao (乌巢), the lightly guarded depot where Yuan Shao stores all his grain and supplies. Burn Wuchao, and Yuan Shao's army will collapse.

Cao Cao acts immediately. He personally leads five thousand picked cavalry on a nighttime forced march to Wuchao. The garrison, under the negligent and drunken general Chunyu Qiong, is caught completely off guard. Cao Cao himself draws his sword and fights in the assault. The fires consume tens of thousands of bushels of grain and all of Yuan Shao's supplies. Cao Cao loads his horses with captured provisions and withdraws triumphantly.

The aftermath is swift. Liu Bei's position at his small stronghold near Gucheng falls to Cao Cao's advancing forces, and Liu Bei flees south to Jingzhou to seek refuge with Liu Biao. Despite opposition from the powerful Cai Mao, Liu Biao receives Liu Bei warmly, and the two bond over their shared imperial lineage. Liu Biao assigns Liu Bei to garrison the small frontier town of Xinye. It is a humble posting, and Liu Bei departs with a heavy heart, while Cai Mao orders his men to watch Guan Yu, Zhang Fei, and Zhao Yun closely.

**Historical significance:** The night raid on Wuchao (乌巢) in the autumn of 200 AD was the decisive turning point of Guandu and one of the most celebrated surprise attacks in Chinese military history. The historical details largely match the dramatization: Xu You revealed Wuchao's location, Cao Cao personally led the strike, and Chunyu Qiong's garrison was overwhelmed. The series compresses subsequent events: historically, Liu Bei did not go to Jingzhou immediately after Guandu. He remained in Yuan Shao's orbit for a time before making his way south. Liu Biao's reception of Liu Bei and the assignment to Xinye are historical, as is Cai Mao's hostility. The Wuchao raid stands as proof of a principle that resonates throughout the Three Kingdoms: in war, logistics and intelligence matter more than raw numbers.
