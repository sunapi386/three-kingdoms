## Episode 35: The Strategist's First Command

Liu Bei asks Zhuge Liang to take command of the defense against Xiahou Dun. In the war council, Guan Yu and Zhang Fei make no effort to hide their disdain for the untested scholar. Liu Bei silences them, declaring he will follow Zhuge Liang's orders without question. Zhuge Liang devises a strategy, deploying the generals precisely, and they crush Xiahou Dun's forces. Guan Yu and Zhang Fei return from the battlefield and bow deeply, their skepticism replaced by respect.

That evening, Zhuge Liang warns Liu Bei that Cao Cao's main army will come next, and Xinye cannot hold. He urges Liu Bei to consider taking Jingzhou, but Liu Bei refuses to seize territory from his kinsman Liu Biao. When the ailing Liu Biao dies, Cai Mao's faction forges an edict installing the younger son Liu Cong as successor, bypassing the legitimate heir Liu Qi. The political situation collapses: Liu Cong, manipulated by Cai Mao and his sister Lady Cai, surrenders Jingzhou to Cao Cao without a fight. Liu Bei, betrayed and outflanked, has no choice but to abandon Xinye and redirect his march toward Liu Qi's base at Jiangling.

**Historical significance:** Zhuge Liang's brilliant debut battle is one of the novel's most significant inventions. The historical Battle of Bowang (博望坡) in 202 AD was actually won by Liu Bei himself, years before Zhuge Liang joined his service. The novel reassigned the credit to Zhuge Liang to establish him as a military genius from his very first engagement. Liu Biao's death, the succession dispute, and Liu Cong's surrender to Cao Cao are all historical. Liu Bei's refusal to seize Jingzhou (whether from genuine principle or political calculation) is recorded in the sources and remains debated by historians.
