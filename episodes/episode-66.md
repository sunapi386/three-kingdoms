## Episode 66: The Phoenix Falls at Fallen Phoenix Slope

Liu Bei's army advances toward Luocheng, but the road is treacherous. Pang Tong, whose courtesy name "Fengchu" means "Young Phoenix," recognizes that the narrow valley called Fallen Phoenix Slope (Luofeng Po) is a perfect site for an ambush. Rather than avoid it, Pang Tong makes a fateful decision: he borrows Liu Bei's distinctive horse, the Dilu ("Leaping Steed"), and insists on leading the column through the pass himself. Zhang Ren's archers lie in wait. When they see the famous horse, they assume its rider is Liu Bei and unleash a storm of arrows. Pang Tong is struck dozens of times and falls from the saddle, dying in the valley. He has left behind a letter for Liu Bei explaining that he chose this death deliberately: by sacrificing himself, he has given Liu Bei the righteous cause ("Liu Zhang murdered my advisor") needed to wage all-out war for Yi Province.

Liu Bei is overcome with grief and rage. He swears to take Yi Province to honor Pang Tong's sacrifice. In Jing Province, Zhuge Liang receives word of Pang Tong's death and decides he must personally lead reinforcements into the west. Before departing, Zhuge Liang entrusts the great seal of Jing Province to Guan Yu with a crucial admonition: "Resist Cao Cao to the north, maintain the alliance with Sun Quan to the east." In Chengdu, Liu Zhang is alarmed by Liu Bei's advance. His advisor Huang Quan suggests ceding territory to Zhang Lu in exchange for military support. Zhang Lu eagerly agrees and dispatches Ma Chao with thirty thousand elite troops to attack Jiameng Pass. Zhang Fei volunteers to meet Ma Chao in battle, and the two legendary warriors clash from daybreak until nightfall in one of the great duels of the series.

**Historical significance:** Pang Tong's death at Fallen Phoenix Slope in 214 CE is one of the most famous tragedies in Three Kingdoms lore. Historically, Pang Tong was killed by a stray arrow during the siege of Luocheng, and the name "Fallen Phoenix Slope" was likely attached to the site retroactively. The show's version, where Pang Tong engineers his own martyrdom, is pure dramatic invention but captures the novel's theme of loyalty unto death. Zhuge Liang's transfer of Jing Province to Guan Yu is a pivotal historical moment: the eight-word instruction ("north resist Cao Cao, east ally Sun Quan") would become tragically ironic when Guan Yu violated the second half. Zhang Fei versus Ma Chao is a celebrated fictional duel from the novel.
