## Episode 90: The Fall of Cao Zhen

Zhuge Liang's health is visibly declining, and the physical toll of years of campaigning in harsh mountain terrain is beginning to show. Despite his condition, he orders the Shu army to withdraw from Chencang, but not before transferring all the grain stores to the Shu main depot at Qishan for future use. He calculates that the rainy season will create an opportunity.

Cao Zhen, emboldened by what he sees as another Shu retreat, divides his forces to reoccupy the abandoned positions. He takes personal command at Chencang, sending triumphant reports back to the Wei capital. His son Cao Shuang distinguishes himself in the pursuit, impressing even the Shu generals with his aggressiveness. But Cao Zhen's success is a trap. With his garrison spread thin across multiple positions, the rainy season arrives. Chencang, sitting in low ground, floods. Grain stores rot, weapons rust, and the troops suffer.

Zhuge Liang strikes at exactly the right moment, leading his forces back through the rain to assault Chencang. Cao Zhen, shocked by the reversal, desperately calls for reinforcements from his scattered garrisons, but they cannot reach him in time. The city falls. Cao Zhen is killed in the chaos of battle, thrown from his horse in the fighting. At the critical moment, Sima Yi arrives with a relief force and stabilizes the Wei lines, preventing the defeat from becoming a total collapse. Emperor Cao Rui, surveying the wreckage, has no choice but to elevate Sima Yi to Grand Commander of all Wei forces. With Cao Zhen dead, the last of the old Cao clan military leaders is gone, and Sima Yi now holds the very position that the Caos had spent years trying to deny him.

**Historical significance:** The historical Cao Zhen died of illness in 231 CE, not in battle. In the novel, Zhuge Liang sends a mocking letter that enrages Cao Zhen so severely that he dies of fury, a literary embellishment. The show takes even greater dramatic license by killing him in combat at Chencang. The real significance of Cao Zhen's death, however the series chooses to portray it, was strategic: it removed the most capable Cao family general and made Sima Yi's assumption of supreme military command inevitable. Cao Shuang, Cao Zhen's son, would later become regent, and his incompetent rule gave Sima Yi the opening to seize power in the Incident at Gaoping Tombs (249 CE), ultimately leading to the Sima family replacing the Cao dynasty entirely. This episode thus plants the seed of Wei's eventual transformation into the Jin dynasty.
