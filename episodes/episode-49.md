## Episode 49: Zhao Yun Takes Guiyang

The campaign to secure the four southern commanderies of Jingzhou continues. Zhang Fei is dispatched to take Wuling, which falls quickly. The main dramatic focus is on Zhao Yun's expedition against Guiyang.

At the walls of Guiyang, the defending general Chen Ying rides out to challenge Zhao Yun in single combat. Zhao Yun, wielding his legendary Qinggang Sword (青釭剑), defeats Chen Ying decisively but spares his life, sending him back into the city. Chen Ying, deeply impressed by Zhao Yun's martial prowess and chivalry, persuades the governor Zhao Fan to surrender without further bloodshed. Zhao Fan opens the city gates and welcomes Liu Bei's forces.

However, Zhao Fan's surrender conceals a trap. Acting on the advice of his counselor Bao Long, Zhao Fan attempts to get Zhao Yun drunk at a banquet and then proposes that they ally together to defect to Cao Cao. Zhao Yun sees through the scheme. With the help of Chen Ying, who has now genuinely switched his loyalty, Zhao Yun subdues the conspirators and secures Guiyang for Liu Bei.

With three of the four commanderies now in hand, Guan Yu arrives from Jingzhou and demands the honor of taking the final one: Changsha. Zhuge Liang, knowing exactly how to motivate Guan Yu, uses reverse psychology, expressing doubt about whether Guan Yu can handle the assignment. Guan Yu, pride stoked, pledges to take Changsha or die trying.

**Historical significance:** The conquest of the four southern commanderies (Lingling, Guiyang, Wuling, and Changsha) is briefly mentioned in the Sanguozhi as occurring after Liu Bei established himself in Jingzhou, but the historical record provides almost no details about how each was taken. The individual adventures of Zhao Yun at Guiyang, Zhang Fei at Wuling, and Guan Yu at Changsha are almost entirely novelistic inventions by Luo Guanzhong, designed to give each of Liu Bei's great generals a showcase moment. The historical Zhao Fan did surrender Guiyang, and there is a tradition that he offered his widowed sister-in-law in marriage to Zhao Yun, who refused, though the details vary between sources.
