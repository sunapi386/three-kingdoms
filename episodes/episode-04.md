## Episode 4: Guan Yu Slays Hua Xiong (温酒斩华雄)

After parting ways with Chen Gong, Cao Cao travels to Chenliu, where he raises an army using his family's wealth. He then sends out a call to arms, summoning the lords of the realm to form a coalition against Dong Zhuo. Eighteen regional warlords answer, assembling a grand alliance under the nominal leadership of Yuan Shao, the most aristocratic among them. The alliance gathers at Hulao Pass, where Dong Zhuo's forces are entrenched.

Dong Zhuo's champion general Hua Xiong proves devastatingly effective, cutting down several coalition commanders in single combat. Yuan Shu's officer Yu She is killed. The warlord Han Fu sends his boasted champion Pan Feng to face Hua Xiong, only to have Pan Feng slain in a single exchange. Panic spreads through the allied camp as no one dares volunteer to fight next.

At this point Guan Yu, an unknown warrior serving the equally obscure Liu Bei, steps forward. Yuan Shu mocks him as a mere foot soldier, but Cao Cao intervenes, noting that Guan Yu has the bearing of a hero and pouring him a cup of warm wine as encouragement. Guan Yu rides out, and before the wine has cooled, he returns with Hua Xiong's severed head. The feat stuns the assembled lords and establishes Guan Yu's legend. Cao Cao is further impressed and privately tells his cousin Cao Ren that he intends to recruit the three brothers. In a conversation with Liu Bei that foreshadows the famous "heroes over green plum wine" scene many episodes later, Cao Cao and Liu Bei share their mutual disdain for the coalition's incompetent lords.

**Historical significance:** "Slaying Hua Xiong while the wine is still warm" is one of the most celebrated scenes in the novel and in Chinese popular culture. Historically, however, it was Sun Jian (not Guan Yu) who defeated Hua Xiong. The novel reassigned this feat to Guan Yu to glorify the Liu Bei faction. The eighteen-lord coalition against Dong Zhuo is historical (190 AD), though the novel exaggerates its unity and drama. The coalition actually fell apart quickly due to internal rivalries, which even this dramatized version hints at through the petty squabbling between Yuan Shao and Yuan Shu.
