## Episode 82: Fire Consumes the Linked Camps

Lu Xun finally launches the attack he has been planning for months. Under cover of darkness, Wu soldiers carrying bundles of dry straw infiltrate the gaps between Liu Bei's sprawling camps. The southeastern wind fans the flames through seven hundred li of connected encampments. The screen fills with fire and chaos as Liu Bei's army, caught in the height of summer heat among forests and wooden fortifications, is consumed. Liu Bei barely escapes with his life as his generals sacrifice themselves to buy him time.

In Chengdu, Zhuge Liang has anticipated this disaster. He has already ordered a stone formation (the Eight Trigram Array) placed along the river to slow any Wu pursuit, and dispatches Zhao Yun with a relief force to escort Liu Bei to safety at Baidicheng (White Emperor City). The stone maze confuses and delays Wu forces long enough for Liu Bei to reach the fortress.

Sun Quan, wary of Cao Pi exploiting Wu's exposed northern border, sends Zhuge Jin (Zhuge Liang's brother) to Baidicheng with a remarkable gesture: he returns all captured Shu grain and military equipment and proposes restoring the Sun-Liu alliance. Liu Bei, humiliated and physically broken, coughs blood at the sight of the envoy. But the next day, with what remains of his strategic mind still functioning, he agrees to the renewed alliance. He begins making arrangements for what he now knows is coming: his own death. He summons his son Liu Shan, his minister Li Yan, and begins the process of entrusting the future of Shu-Han to others.

**Historical significance:** The Battle of Yiling (also called the Battle of Xiaoting) in the summer of 222 CE was a catastrophic defeat for Shu. The fire attack is historical, as is Liu Bei's retreat to Baidicheng. The stone formation (八阵图) that supposedly blocked Lu Xun is a novel invention, though stone arrangements attributed to Zhuge Liang did exist as landmarks. The return of captured supplies and the peace overture from Wu are based on real events: Sun Quan genuinely feared a Wei attack from the north and needed to stabilize the Shu border quickly. Liu Bei never left Baidicheng after this retreat. He remained there, declining in health, until his death the following spring.
