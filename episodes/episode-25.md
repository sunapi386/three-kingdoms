## Episode 25: A Thousand Li Ride Alone

Guan Yu receives Liu Bei's letter and immediately seeks an audience with Cao Cao to take his leave, but Cao Cao hangs an "Unavailable" sign on his gate and refuses to see him. Zhang Liao likewise turns Guan Yu away. Undeterred, Guan Yu returns to his residence, informs Liu Bei's two wives of the situation, formally returns his seal of office and all the gold Cao Cao has given him, and rides out of the city.

In a famous scene, Cao Cao has a change of heart and personally leads his generals to see Guan Yu off at the city outskirts. He presents Guan Yu with gold and a brocade robe as farewell gifts. Guan Yu, deeply moved, dismounts and bows to Cao Cao in gratitude before departing. Watching him go, Cao Cao realizes the terrifying power of true loyalty.

Meanwhile, Zhang Fei has taken refuge in a small city, where he has overthrown the local magistrate and installed himself as ruler. He recruits and trains a scrappy fighting force while sending scouts to search for Liu Bei and Guan Yu. When he learns that Guan Yu has been serving under Cao Cao, Zhang Fei erupts in fury and weeps with rage. Along the road, Guan Yu begins his legendary journey of "crossing five passes and slaying six generals" (过五关斩六将) as he fights his way through Cao Cao's territory, escorting Liu Bei's wives to safety.

**Historical significance:** Guan Yu's departure from Cao Cao's service is historical; the Sanguozhi confirms he left to rejoin Liu Bei after learning of his brother's location. However, the dramatic "hanging up the seal and returning the gold" (挂印封金) is a novelistic embellishment, and the famous "crossing five passes, slaying six generals" sequence is entirely fictional. No such gauntlet of checkpoints existed in history. The novel invented this legendary journey to cement Guan Yu's image as the supreme embodiment of loyalty. In the series, these scenes are visually spectacular and emotionally resonant, representing the climax of Guan Yu's moral arc within Cao Cao's camp.
