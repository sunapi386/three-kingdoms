# New Three Kingdoms (新三国) - Episodes 31-40
*From the Three Visits to the Eve of Red Cliffs*

This batch of episodes traces one of the most dramatic arcs in the entire series: Liu Bei's desperate search for a strategist, the recruitment of Zhuge Liang, the catastrophic flight from Cao Cao's southern invasion, and the frantic diplomacy that forges the Sun-Liu alliance just in time to face the greatest military threat any of them have known. By episode 40, the pieces are set for the Battle of Red Cliffs.

---

## Episode 31: The Leap at Tan Stream

Liu Bei is awakened in the night by Liu Qi, who warns him that Cai Mao has sent assassins. Liu Bei barely escapes the ambush at his quarters. Cai Mao, seizing on a silk cloth Liu Bei dropped during his flight, forges a treasonous poem on it and presents it to Liu Biao, who is initially enraged and orders an attack on Xinye. But Liu Qi exposes the inconsistencies, and Liu Biao realizes Cai Mao's deception.

Days later, Liu Bei presides over a temple gathering on Liu Biao's behalf, meeting officials from nine commanderies. Liu Qi, trembling, warns him that Cai Mao's elite guards are poised to strike. Liu Bei mounts his horse, the legendary Di Lu, and flees. Cornered at the banks of Tan Stream with pursuers closing in, the horse stumbles into the water. Then, in a moment that becomes legend, Di Lu surges forward and leaps across the stream to safety.

On the far bank, Liu Bei finds shelter with the recluse Shuijing Xiansheng (the "Water Mirror" master, Sima Hui), who offers counsel and recommends a brilliant young strategist: a man named Xu Shu. Liu Bei returns to Xinye with renewed hope, and Xu Shu enters his service just as Cao Cao prepares a new offensive.

**Historical significance:** The "leaping the Tan Stream" incident is one of the most celebrated scenes from the novel *Romance of the Three Kingdoms* and has very little historical basis. The Records of the Three Kingdoms (Sanguozhi) mention Liu Bei's time in Jingzhou under Liu Biao and the political tensions with the Cai clan, but the dramatic horse leap is novelistic embellishment. Sima Hui's recommendation of talented scholars, however, does appear in historical sources. This episode establishes the transition from Liu Bei as a wandering lord dependent on others' hospitality to a leader actively seeking the advisor who will change his fate.

---

## Episode 32: Xu Shu's Farewell

Cao Ren arrives at Fancheng eager for glory and deploys the Eight Trigrams formation against Liu Bei's forces. Following Xu Shu's advice to hold steady, Guan Yu, Zhang Fei, and Zhao Yun exploit the formation's weaknesses and rout Cao Ren. Humiliated, Cao Ren attempts a night raid on Xinye but walks into another of Xu Shu's traps, suffering a devastating defeat.

Back at his main camp, Cao Cao is impressed by Xu Shu's brilliance and devises a cruel stratagem: he takes Xu Shu's elderly mother hostage and forges a letter summoning Xu Shu to her side. Xu Shu, filial to his core, cannot refuse. Despite the pleas of Liu Bei and his brothers, he departs. But he turns back one final time to recommend the one person who surpasses him: Zhuge Liang, the "Sleeping Dragon" of Longzhong. Along his journey, Xu Shu stops to beg Zhuge Liang to serve Liu Bei, but Zhuge Liang refuses. When Xu Shu arrives at Cao Cao's camp, he discovers the letter was forged; his mother was never in danger until now. Xu Shu's mother, furious that her son abandoned a righteous lord for a tyrant's trick, hangs herself. From that day forward, Xu Shu serves Cao Cao in name only, never offering a single word of counsel.

**Historical significance:** The historical Xu Shu (originally named Shan Fu) did serve Liu Bei briefly and did leave for Cao Cao's side after his mother was captured, but the timeline differs from the show. In actual history, Xu Shu's mother was seized during the chaos of the Changban retreat in 208 AD, not through a forged letter beforehand. The novel invented the forged letter, the dramatic farewell, and the mother's suicide to heighten the tragedy. The phrase "Xu Shu in Cao's camp, not a single word offered" (徐庶进曹营，一言不发) became a Chinese proverb for someone present in body but absent in spirit.

## Episode 33: Three Visits to the Thatched Cottage

After Xu Shu's departure, Liu Bei resolves to seek out Zhuge Liang at his retreat in Longzhong. Guan Yu and Zhang Fei are skeptical that any recluse scholar is worth such effort. On the first visit, Zhuge Liang is away traveling, and they return empty-handed. Along the road back to Xinye, they encounter Cui Zhouping, one of Zhuge Liang's associates, whose intellect deeply impresses Liu Bei and only strengthens his determination.

Winter sets in. Word arrives that Zhuge Liang has returned from his travels, and Liu Bei sets out again through harsh weather. The second visit also fails. On the third attempt, Zhang Fei's impatience nearly ruins everything when he accidentally starts a fire that wakes the sleeping Zhuge Liang. But this time, Liu Bei finally meets the man face to face. The two sit down together, and Zhuge Liang lays out his vision for dividing the empire into three realms, with Liu Bei claiming the lands of Shu in the west. This is the Longzhong Plan, and it will become the strategic blueprint for everything that follows.

**Historical significance:** The "Three Visits to the Thatched Cottage" (三顾茅庐) is among the most famous episodes in all of Chinese literature and culture. It is also one of the better-attested events from this period. Zhuge Liang himself references the three visits in his own memorial, the Chu Shi Biao (出师表), writing that Liu Bei "lowered himself to visit my thatched cottage three times." The Longzhong Plan (隆中对) is recorded in the Sanguozhi and represents genuine strategic thinking: Zhuge Liang advised taking Jingzhou and Yi Province (Sichuan), allying with Sun Quan against Cao Cao, and waiting for the right moment to reunify the empire. The show dramatizes the conversations and adds color to the failed visits, but the core event is historically grounded.

---

## Episode 34: Sun Ce's Legacy

Zhuge Liang's words strike Liu Bei like a revelation. Unable to resist Liu Bei's sincerity any longer, Zhuge Liang agrees to leave his cottage and serve as advisor. The scene shifts east to Wu, where Sun Ce, the young conqueror who built the Sun family's domain south of the Yangtze, is ambushed by assassins during a hunting expedition and mortally wounded.

Sun Quan rushes back to the general's residence. Following Zhang Zhao's instructions, he seals the roads and suppresses the news. On his deathbed, Sun Ce entrusts the governance of the three river provinces to his younger brother. Sun Quan, only eighteen years old, inherits a territory surrounded by enemies on three sides and staffed entirely by his father's and brother's veterans. Zhou Yu, stationed at the front line in Baqiu, races back to mourn. In the funeral hall, he declines Sun Quan's offer of supreme command but pledges lifelong loyalty to the young lord.

Meanwhile, in Xinye, Liu Bei treats Zhuge Liang with the reverence due a teacher, spending his days in deep discussion. Guan Yu and Zhang Fei, jealous of this newcomer's influence, simmer with resentment. Then word arrives: Cao Cao has dispatched Xiahou Dun with an army aimed straight at Xinye.

**Historical significance:** Sun Ce's assassination in 200 AD is well documented in historical records, though the show compresses the timeline by placing it alongside Zhuge Liang's recruitment (historically 207 AD). Sun Ce was genuinely ambushed by retainers of Xu Gong, a local official he had killed. His deathbed charge to Sun Quan and Zhou Yu's loyalty pledge are grounded in the historical sources, though the specific dialogue is dramatized. The show's placement of these events together serves a narrative purpose: it introduces Sun Quan as a ruler in the same breath that Liu Bei gains his strategist, setting up the eventual alliance between the two.

---

## Episode 35: The Strategist's First Command

Liu Bei asks Zhuge Liang to take command of the defense against Xiahou Dun. In the war council, Guan Yu and Zhang Fei make no effort to hide their disdain for the untested scholar. Liu Bei silences them, declaring he will follow Zhuge Liang's orders without question. Zhuge Liang devises a strategy, deploying the generals precisely, and they crush Xiahou Dun's forces. Guan Yu and Zhang Fei return from the battlefield and bow deeply, their skepticism replaced by respect.

That evening, Zhuge Liang warns Liu Bei that Cao Cao's main army will come next, and Xinye cannot hold. He urges Liu Bei to consider taking Jingzhou, but Liu Bei refuses to seize territory from his kinsman Liu Biao. When the ailing Liu Biao dies, Cai Mao's faction forges an edict installing the younger son Liu Cong as successor, bypassing the legitimate heir Liu Qi. The political situation collapses: Liu Cong, manipulated by Cai Mao and his sister Lady Cai, surrenders Jingzhou to Cao Cao without a fight. Liu Bei, betrayed and outflanked, has no choice but to abandon Xinye and redirect his march toward Liu Qi's base at Jiangling.

**Historical significance:** Zhuge Liang's brilliant debut battle is one of the novel's most significant inventions. The historical Battle of Bowang (博望坡) in 202 AD was actually won by Liu Bei himself, years before Zhuge Liang joined his service. The novel reassigned the credit to Zhuge Liang to establish him as a military genius from his very first engagement. Liu Biao's death, the succession dispute, and Liu Cong's surrender to Cao Cao are all historical. Liu Bei's refusal to seize Jingzhou (whether from genuine principle or political calculation) is recorded in the sources and remains debated by historians.

## Episode 36: Changban

In Cao Cao's camp, Liu Cong and Cai Mao arrive. Cao Cao rewards Cai Mao with command of the captured Jingzhou naval forces. Liu Cong and his mother are "escorted" to Qingzhou to attend the puppet Emperor Xian, effectively prisoners.

When Cao Cao learns that Liu Bei is fleeing south toward Jiangling with over a hundred thousand civilian refugees, he orders an immediate pursuit. The great flight reaches its crisis at Changban Slope. In the chaos, Zhao Yun discovers Lady Mi gravely wounded, sheltering the infant Liu Shan (A Dou). She entrusts the child to Zhao Yun and throws herself into a dry well to avoid slowing him down. Zhao Yun, cradling the baby against his armor, fights his way through wave after wave of Cao Cao's soldiers. Cao Cao, watching from a hilltop and admiring Zhao Yun's valor, orders his men to capture the warrior alive rather than kill him, which inadvertently gives Zhao Yun the opening he needs to break free.

Zhang Fei arrives to cover the retreat. At Changban Bridge, he positions himself alone with a handful of riders behind him. His thunderous shout echoes across the water, and Cao Cao's vanguard freezes, suspecting an ambush. Cao Cao hesitates and orders a withdrawal. But Zhang Fei then destroys the bridge, which reveals there was no ambush at all, prompting Cao Cao to resume the chase. Liu Bei reaches the river crossing at Jianjin with enemies closing from behind and open water ahead. At the last possible moment, boats appear on the river: Liu Qi has come from Jiangling with a rescue fleet. Liu Bei escapes across the water while Cao Cao watches from the shore.

**Historical significance:** The Battle of Changban (长坂之战, 208 AD) is one of the most historically attested events in this sequence. The Sanguozhi confirms that Zhao Yun protected the infant Liu Shan through the rout, that Zhang Fei held the bridge with a small force and intimidated Cao Cao's army into hesitation, and that Liu Bei fled with a massive column of civilians. However, the novel and the show heavily dramatize the details. Lady Mi's self-sacrifice by throwing herself into a well is fictional. Zhao Yun's "seven charges in, seven charges out" is novelistic exaggeration of what was likely a desperate but less cinematic escape. Zhang Fei's bridge stand, by contrast, is one of the rare cases where the historical account is already dramatic enough that the novel barely needed to embellish it. The episode captures the pivotal moment when Liu Bei's cause nearly died, and the loyalty of his followers is what saved it.

## Episode 37: Tongue Battle with the Scholars

Cao Cao orders his armies to rest and refit while Cai Mao trains the Jingzhou naval forces on Poyang Lake, preparing for a final push south to destroy Sun Quan. In Jiangling, Liu Bei's position is precarious. Then Lu Su arrives from the Southland, ostensibly to pay respects to the late Liu Biao. Zhuge Liang sees the opportunity immediately and volunteers to accompany Lu Su back east to persuade Sun Quan to ally against Cao Cao.

On the journey, Lu Su repeatedly warns Zhuge Liang: "Whatever you do, do not mention how large Cao Cao's army is." Zhuge Liang smiles and agrees. Lu Su enters Sun Quan's court first, where he finds the elder statesman Zhang Zhao already leading the surrender faction, arguing that Cao Cao's million-strong host cannot be resisted. Sun Quan wavers. Lu Su delivers the decisive blow in private: if the officials surrender, Cao Cao will still employ them to govern the south, but if Sun Quan himself surrenders, he will be caged in the imperial capital of Xuchang for the rest of his life.

The next day, Lu Su escorts Zhuge Liang into the audience hall. In the outer corridor, more than twenty of Wu's sharpest scholars and advisors wait to challenge him. What follows is the legendary "tongue battle with the scholars" (舌战群儒). One by one, Zhang Zhao, Gu Yong, Yu Fan, Lu Ji, and Luo Tong attack Zhuge Liang's positions, and one by one he demolishes their arguments. When Zhuge Liang finally meets Sun Quan in the inner hall, he employs a deliberate provocation, goading the young lord until Sun Quan storms out in fury.

**Historical significance:** Zhuge Liang's embassy to Sun Quan is historical. The Sanguozhi records that Lu Su advocated the alliance and that Zhuge Liang traveled east to persuade Sun Quan. However, the spectacular "tongue battle with the scholars" is a novel invention, one of the most celebrated rhetorical set pieces in Chinese literature. The historical Zhuge Liang did argue the case for alliance, but the dramatic sequential demolition of each opponent is fiction. Lu Su's private argument to Sun Quan about the difference between his officials' fate and his own is also from the novel, though it captures a genuine political reality: Sun Quan personally had the most to lose from surrender.

---

## Episode 38: Zhou Yu Returns

Lu Su scolds Zhuge Liang for insulting Sun Quan, but Zhuge Liang calmly instructs Lu Su to tell the young lord that he has a strategy to defeat Cao Cao. Sun Quan's anger turns to curiosity. He agrees to convene a full war council the following day.

That night, Zhang Zhao visits Sun Quan privately and argues again for surrender, shaking Sun Quan's resolve once more. But Zhou Yu, who has been away commanding the frontier forces, races back to Chaisang upon hearing the news. As Wu's various officials visit Zhou Yu that evening, each pressing either for war or surrender, Zhou Yu remains deliberately ambiguous, revealing nothing. Finally, Lu Su brings Zhuge Liang to meet Zhou Yu. The two brilliant strategists size each other up carefully. Zhou Yu announces that in his view, war means certain defeat and surrender is the wiser course. Lu Su is horrified. But Zhuge Liang, reading the game, deliberately misquotes the "Ode to the Bronze Sparrow Terrace," claiming Cao Cao's true ambition in conquering the south is to seize the two legendary beauties, the Qiao sisters, one of whom is Zhou Yu's own wife. Zhou Yu, whether genuinely enraged or simply finding the provocation he needed, drops the mask.

The next morning, before the assembled court, Zhou Yu lays out the four critical military blunders Cao Cao has committed and declares that fifty thousand elite troops will be enough to destroy him. Sun Quan draws his sword, slashes the corner of his desk, and declares: anyone who speaks of surrender again will be treated like this table. He appoints Zhou Yu as Grand Commander of all Wu's forces.

**Historical significance:** Zhou Yu's return and his role as the architect of resistance is well attested in history. The Sanguozhi makes clear that Zhou Yu was the primary advocate for war and that Sun Quan's decision to fight was heavily influenced by his analysis. The "Bronze Sparrow Terrace" provocation, however, is a famous anachronism in the novel: the poem (铜雀台赋) was written by Cao Zhi after the Battle of Red Cliffs, not before, making Zhuge Liang's use of it a narrative impossibility that the novel employs for dramatic effect. The show faithfully reproduces this beloved scene. Sun Quan's sword-on-table declaration is from the novel. Historically, the decision to resist was real, but the theatrical gesture is dramatized.

## Episode 39: Jiang Gan Steals a Letter

Zhuge Liang's brother Zhuge Jin, sent by Zhou Yu, visits the Southland camp to persuade Zhuge Liang to defect to Wu's service. Before he can even make the pitch, Zhuge Liang turns the conversation around and urges his brother to join Liu Bei instead. Zhuge Jin returns empty-handed, and Zhou Yu's determination to eliminate Zhuge Liang only grows.

Meanwhile, Liu Bei grows anxious about Zhuge Liang's prolonged absence in the east and sends messengers. Zhuge Liang replies with a cryptic instruction: have Zhao Yun bring a small boat to the southern shore each day after the twentieth of the eleventh month.

On Cao Cao's side, the question of how to break the Sun-Liu alliance leads to a volunteer: Jiang Gan, a scholar who claims to be an old classmate of Zhou Yu's. He crosses the river to Zhou Yu's camp, confident he can talk his former friend into switching sides. Zhou Yu sees through the ploy instantly and turns it into an opportunity. He throws an extravagant banquet, feigns drunkenness, and "accidentally" leaves forged documents in his tent that implicate Cao Cao's naval commanders Cai Mao and Zhang Yun as secret agents working for Wu. Jiang Gan, believing he has stumbled onto priceless intelligence, steals the letters and hurries back to Cao Cao's camp.

**Historical significance:** Jiang Gan was a real person who did visit Zhou Yu, but the historical account says only that Zhou Yu refused to be swayed and that Jiang Gan returned praising Zhou Yu's character. The entire "stealing a letter" ruse is a novel invention, one of the most famous counter-intelligence plots in Chinese literature. The real Cai Mao and Zhang Yun did serve Cao Cao after the surrender of Jingzhou, but whether they were executed and the circumstances differ from the novel's version. This episode showcases the novel's fascination with deception and psychological warfare, themes that dominate the entire Red Cliffs sequence.

---

## Episode 40: Borrowing Arrows with Straw Boats

Cao Cao reads Jiang Gan's stolen documents and, in a fit of rage, orders the execution of Cai Mao and Zhang Yun, his two most experienced naval commanders. He realizes the deception only after the deed is done. Desperate to replace them, Cao Cao appoints Yu Jin to command the fleet. Yu Jin's solution to the northern soldiers' seasickness is to chain all the warships together, creating a massive floating platform. Cao Cao approves, convinced this guarantees stability for the coming battle.

When Zhou Yu learns that Cai Mao and Zhang Yun are dead, he is elated but suspects Zhuge Liang also saw through the scheme from the start. He sends Lu Su to probe Zhuge Liang, and his suspicions are confirmed. Viewing Zhuge Liang as too dangerous to leave alive, Zhou Yu devises a trap disguised as a military assignment: produce one hundred thousand arrows in ten days, an impossible task meant to justify execution when it fails. Zhuge Liang accepts, but raises the stakes, promising to deliver in three days.

On a night of dense fog, Zhuge Liang orders a fleet of boats loaded with bundles of straw and straw-stuffed scarecrows to sail toward Cao Cao's position. In the fog, Cao Cao's archers, unable to see clearly, unleash volley after volley into what they assume is an attack force. The arrows thud into the straw by the tens of thousands. By dawn, Zhuge Liang's boats return laden with arrows, the quota more than met. Zhou Yu can only marvel. Afterward, Zhuge Liang and Zhou Yu independently arrive at the same conclusion: fire is the key to defeating Cao Cao's chained fleet. The Cai brothers (relatives of the executed Cai Mao) arrive at Zhou Yu's camp seeking to serve as double agents for Cao Cao. Zhou Yu welcomes them, recognizing that spies can be turned to his advantage. The stage is now set for the great battle.

**Historical significance:** The "straw boat arrow borrowing" stratagem (草船借箭) is one of the novel's most iconic inventions, and it is entirely fictional as attributed to Zhuge Liang. The historical kernel comes from a much later incident: in 213 AD, Sun Quan himself sailed close to Cao Cao's camp on a reconnaissance mission, and Cao Cao's archers filled his boat with arrows until it listed to one side, at which point Sun Quan turned the boat around so the other side would be struck, balancing the weight. The novel transplanted and transformed this anecdote, giving it to Zhuge Liang and setting it before Red Cliffs. The chaining of warships is mentioned in historical accounts and is the critical tactical error that made the fire attack possible. By the end of this episode, every element of the Battle of Red Cliffs is in place: the chained ships, the fire strategy, the double agents, and the alliance that will change the course of Chinese history.
